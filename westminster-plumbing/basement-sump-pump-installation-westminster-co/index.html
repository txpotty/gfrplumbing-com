    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Westminster

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Cutting a new pit through the slab typically costs $1,500 to $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. The backup is a common add.

    Most standard installs finish within 4 to 8 hours. Long discharge runs or a new pit can add several hours, and if we need a new circuit from the panel, an electrician may need to come out the same day, which can push the job into the evening. The timeline comes with the quote.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. The secondary, usually battery powered, sits beside it as a backup, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Each pump gets its own check valve.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.
