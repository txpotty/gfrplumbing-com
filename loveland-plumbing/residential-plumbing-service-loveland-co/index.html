    Frequently Asked Questions About Residential Plumbing Service in Loveland

    Small repairs in Loveland typically cost $150 to $300. A main line clog or a new water heater falls more in the $400 to $1,500 range, sometimes higher. The price is set once we see the problem. You get it in writing, and it only changes if you approve.

    Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. Soft water has had most of those minerals removed, usually by an ion-exchange softener, and the difference shows up as scale in water heaters, white crust on faucets, spotty dishes and soap that will not lather well. Soft water is easier on fixtures.

    Once a year is a good rule. Hard water speeds up sediment buildup, so homes with scale may need a flush more often, and a yearly check lets us test the relief valve, look at the anode rod and catch a weeping fitting before it rusts through the jacket. An anode rod often wears out in three to five years.

    Soft water and normal pressure prevent a lot of what we see. Keep grease out of the drains and test pressure once a year, since anything over 80 psi wears out supply hoses and fixtures faster than people expect. A yearly inspection catches the rest.

    Moen, Delta, Kohler, American Standard, Pfister and TOTO make up most of our installs. Fixtures you bought yourself are fine too, as long as it is a complete unit with the right connections, and we will check the box before the appointment so nothing is missing when we arrive. Bring the receipt.

    Tightening a loose connection or plunging a toilet is fine. For anything bigger, we usually suggest calling a pro, because a bad sweat joint, a cross-threaded fitting or a gas connection done wrong can cost far more than the original repair would have. Gas work in particular should go to a licensed plumber.
