    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Loveland

    Small indoor hydromechanical traps usually cost $1,500 to $5,000 installed. Large outdoor gravity interceptors often run $8,000 to $30,000 or more with excavation, concrete and site restoration. We provide a written estimate after a site visit.

    Most food service businesses that cook, wash dishes or prepare food with fats, oils or grease are required to have one. Local pretreatment programs set the specific rules, and we can help you find out what applies to your site.

    Installation time depends on the size and complexity of the grease trap. A small under-sink unit might take a few hours, while a large outdoor interceptor could require several days due to excavation and concrete work. We aim to complete installations efficiently to minimize disruption to your business operations.

    The primary purpose of a grease trap is to intercept fats, oils, and greases (FOG) from commercial kitchen wastewater before they enter the public sewer system. This prevents pipe blockages, protects municipal infrastructure, and helps businesses comply with environmental regulations.

    While the terms are often used interchangeably, 'grease trap' typically refers to smaller, indoor hydro-mechanical units, often found under sinks. 'Grease interceptor' usually refers to larger, outdoor gravity-fed units with higher capacities. Both serve the same purpose of separating FOG from wastewater.

    We recommend and install high-quality, durable grease trap brands such as Zurn, Big Dipper, Schier, and Rockford Separators. These manufacturers are known for their reliability and compliance with industry standards, offering various solutions to meet diverse commercial kitchen needs.

    Indoor traps should be close to the fixtures they serve and easy to open for cleaning. Outdoor interceptors are usually placed where a pump truck can reach them without blocking customer traffic.

    Signs you might need a new grease trap include frequent sewer backups, persistent foul odors coming from drains, slow drainage throughout your kitchen, or if your current unit is visibly corroded or leaking. If your existing trap is old and no longer meets current local codes in Loveland, an upgrade is also advisable.
