    CSST Repair and Bonding in Loveland, CO

    Newer homes often have corrugated stainless steel tubing running through the attic. CSST can be damaged by nails, rodents or lightning, and older yellow-jacket CSST needs proper bonding to the electrical grounding system to reduce the risk of a lightning arc. It takes about an hour. Damaged runs get spliced with the manufacturer's fittings, and we confirm the bond on the way out. Sudden summer thunderstorms bring flash flooding and hail, and heavy runoff can overwhelm storm drains and sewer lines.

    Natural Gas Leak Detection and Repair in Loveland

    Finding a gas leak starts at the meter. With every appliance off, a moving test dial on the meter tells us gas is escaping somewhere, and then we isolate branches to narrow it down. An electronic detector does the rest. Soap solution at the suspect fitting confirms it with bubbles, and then we repair the joint, replace the section or swap the connector before testing again.

    Propane Gas Line Service in Loveland, CO

    Propane systems run at different pressures than natural gas and use a two-stage regulator setup between the tank and the house. Regulators fail too. Our work covers propane yard lines, regulator replacement and fitting repairs, plus a check that every appliance has its propane orifices installed. A natural gas burner running on propane will burn rich and soot up. We test the system the same way we test natural gas piping.

    Buried Yard Line Repair and Replacement in Loveland, CO

    Many older homes have steel gas lines running from the meter to the house or out to a pool heater. The wrap fails eventually. Once the coating cracks, the pipe starts to corrode, and wet soil from sprinklers or a low spot in the yard speeds that up a great deal. We call 811 before digging. The new line is polyethylene with tracer wire, or coated steel where it has to be exposed, and we backfill and clean up the yard when the test passes.

    Corroded Gas Pipe Replacement in Loveland

    Buried steel gas line corrodes once its coating cracks, and moisture, roots and ground movement speed it up. Expansive clay soils are a well-known problem in parts of Colorado, swelling with moisture and lifting slabs, basement floors, and buried pipes. We replace corroded sections with polyethylene pipe laid with tracer wire, using an approved transition riser where the line comes up out of the ground at the house. Exposed sections get coated steel. Everything is tested before the trench is backfilled, so a bad joint gets caught while it is still easy to reach.

    Black Iron Pipe Repair in Loveland, CO

Rusted pipe that runs outside gets painted or replaced with coated steel so it does not come back as the same problem.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Repair in Loveland

    Small fitting or connector repairs usually run $150 to $450. Section replacements run $400 to $1,200, and buried line replacement can reach $1,000 to $4,000 or more.

    Most gas piping changes and all new gas lines in Loveland, CO need a permit. Like-for-like connector replacements often skip it. With a permit, an inspector reviews sizing, materials and the test result, and our office files it and handles the visit for you.

    Leaks at the meter or on the utility's service line belong to the gas company. Leave the meter alone. Leave the property and call the utility, and after they address their side we can fix anything on your side of the meter.

    Watch for a sulfur or rotten egg smell, a hiss near a pipe or appliance, visible damage to gas piping or dying plants near an outdoor line. Get outside right away. Once outside, call the gas company and then us, because the utility has to secure the meter before a plumber can safely start looking for the leak.

    Gas lines do not need routine service the way a water heater does. Checks still help. During other plumbing visits, we can look over visible gas piping, connectors and shutoffs and point out old brass connectors, missing sediment traps or rust that is worth fixing before it leaks.

    Gas piping should be repaired by a licensed professional. Mistakes can cause fires, explosions or carbon monoxide problems, and DIY work may not pass inspection.

    Gas repairs that change piping and all new gas lines usually need a permit and inspection. Our office files it and books the inspection.
