    Pedestal Sump Pumps in Longmont

    Older homes often have narrow pits that will not fit a submersible. A pedestal pump fits where nothing else will, and we install it with rigid discharge, a quality check valve, and a float rod set to your pit depth so it starts before water reaches the top of the liner. Noisy, but they last.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Longmont, CO

    Battery backups earn their keep during outages. Drought cycles are common here, so water-efficient fixtures and careful irrigation choices are part of most plumbing upgrades. We add a second pump in the same pit with its own float, wired to a sealed AGM battery on a smart charger that tests itself and beeps when the battery gets weak. Your primary pump still handles the everyday load.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Longmont

    Outages and heavy rain tend to show up together. A battery backup keeps a second pump running without house power, and a fully charged 75 amp-hour battery can run a typical backup pump for several hours of on-and-off cycling, longer on a light night. We test it under load.

    Commercial and Multi-Unit Sump Installations in Longmont

    Duplex systems with two pumps and an alternating panel are standard for parking garages and multi-unit buildings with below-grade levels in Longmont. The panel swaps the lead pump every cycle, so wear stays even. Staff get a high water alarm. We coordinate the wiring with your electrician.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Longmont

    Prices in Longmont, CO usually land between $800 and $2,500. Pedestal pumps cost less than cast iron submersibles, and the biggest swing comes from whether we have to excavate a new pit and whether you want a battery backup added to the same basin. Every quote is itemized.

    Submersibles sit in the pit, sealed, and run quiet. Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, which makes them cheaper and easier to service but louder, so they work best in an unfinished utility room rather than a finished family room. Both move water fine.

    Many newer homes across Colorado use PEX with manifold systems, which handle freezing better than rigid pipe but still require protection. Slab homes without a basement rarely need a pump. Basements and below-grade rooms are a different case, since they can take on water fast during heavy storms, especially where the ground sheds rain rather than soaking it up. A pit costs less than a cleanup.

    Usually not. Most standard policies exclude new pumps and repairs from normal wear, and many also exclude flooding from groundwater unless you bought a water backup or sump failure endorsement for an extra premium. Your agent can tell you what is covered.

    We strongly recommend professional installation. The job combines electrical, plumbing and concrete work, and a bad seal on the lid, an undersized pit or a discharge that drains toward the house can leave you worse off than having no pump at all. Mistakes show up in storms.

    Quality cast iron pumps usually carry three to five years of factory coverage. We back our installation labor separately, and we walk you through the exact terms for the model you pick before we install it.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. Rust stains and a float that sticks are warning signs too, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Replace it before the next storm.
