    Pit Cleaning and Liner Checks in Longmont

    Long dry stretches let dust, sand, leaves and gravel settle at the bottom of the basin. Sooner or later the pump sucks it into the screen and locks up the impeller. Our plumber pumps the basin down, rinses the screen and inspects the liner for cracks that let dirt seep in through the walls. Takes about twenty minutes.

    Elevator Pit and Garage Pumps in Longmont

    Elevator pits, parking garages and mechanical rooms all collect water that has nowhere to go by gravity, so a pump has to lift it out. In elevator pits, the pump usually needs an oil-sensing control so it will not discharge hydraulic fluid. Duplex systems and high-water alarms get tested on a fixed schedule. The property manager gets a copy of every log.

    Float Switch Inspection in Longmont, CO

    The float switch tells the pump when to start and stop, and a float with too little travel between on and off makes the pump short cycle dozens of times an hour. We watch the float through a full cycle. Then we set the on and off points so the pump runs longer, less often.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Maintenance in Longmont, CO

    Backup pumps sit in the pit next to the main pump and run off a 12-volt battery when the power drops or the primary fails. Our plumber load tests the battery instead of just reading its voltage. Then we unplug the main pump and let the backup empty the pit on its own. Alarms get tested too.

    Crawlspace and Window Well Pumps in Longmont, CO

    Not every sump sits in a basement. Plenty of crawlspaces and deep window wells use a small pump to clear storm runoff. Our plumber clears the screen, tests the float and traces the discharge to daylight or an approved drain, since a line that ends under the house only relocates the water. Hard freezes are a normal part of winter in Colorado, and pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, and garages are particularly vulnerable.

    Commercial Sump Pump Maintenance in Longmont, CO

    Commercial pits are often bigger and deeper, and many have two pumps on an alternating controller. Each pump gets tested, along with the alternator and the high-water alarm. You get a written record of each reading, which comes in handy for insurance renewals and inspections. Quarterly visits are common.

    Zoeller Sump Pump Maintenance in Longmont

    Zoeller pumps like the M53 and M63 use cast iron housings and an integral float switch, and they tend to outlast cheaper plastic units. We still check the casting for cracks and the float for free travel. We carry the common Zoeller parts on the truck.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Check-Ups in Longmont, CO

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, so the wear shows up in the shaft, the bearings and the float rod. We check the rod for bends and the stops for slipping. Bearings get a listen. A motor that whines or rattles on startup is usually close to done.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Maintenance in Longmont

    A standard inspection and cleaning usually runs $125 to $250. A new float switch or check valve adds about $75 to $250, depending on parts.

    Seven to ten years is normal for a quality pump. A pump left dry for months can seize early, so testing during dry spells matters.

    Around Longmont, a yearly water heater flush helps clear the scale that collects at the bottom of the tank. In hard-water areas, scale builds up on the impeller, the float rod and the basin walls. Given a few years, the buildup slows the pump and can stick a vertical float so the pump never kicks on. Cleaning it off is part of every visit.

    The check valve stops water in the discharge pipe from dropping back into the pit after the pump shuts off. Without one, the pump ends up moving the same water again and again. All that extra cycling shortens motor life and adds to the power bill.

    We work on Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant, Basement Watchdog, Liberty and most other residential brands. Common floats and seal kits ride on the truck.

    Without a working battery or water-powered backup, the pit keeps filling during an outage and eventually spills over onto the basement floor. During a heavy rain, that can happen in less than an hour. Water-powered backups need city water pressure, so they do not work on a private well.

    Most visits run 45 minutes to an hour. A same-day float or check valve replacement adds a few minutes.
