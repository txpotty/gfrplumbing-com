    Sump Pump Replacement in Longmont, CO

    Floats stick and motors seize when a pump sits idle for months between storms. We pull the old pump and inspect the pit. If the old one could not keep up, we size up. We fill the pit and watch it run.

    Crawl Space Sump Pump Installation in Longmont, CO

    Crawl spaces with standing water breed mold and rot sill plates within a few seasons. We set a basin at the lowest point, run perforated drain pipe to it where needed and seal the vapor barrier around the lid so the space stays dry. Access is often tight. The discharge exits through the rim joist and drains away from the house.

    High Water Alarms and Smart Monitoring in Longmont

    A high water alarm is a float switch that sounds when the pit rises past normal. Wi-Fi models send a text too. Rentals need these. We add them to new installs and to pumps already in place.

    Sump Pump Installation Cost in Longmont, CO

    Price depends mostly on whether a pit already exists. Swapping a pump in an existing pit is the cheapest job, while a new pit means saw cutting, digging, gravel and a concrete patch, which roughly doubles the labor. Battery backups and alarms add to the total. Your written quote shows each option and its price.

    Combination Sump Pump Systems in Longmont

    Combination units pair a primary pump and a battery backup on one pre-plumbed discharge assembly. They fit pits where two separate pumps would crowd each other. That saves some labor. They suit homes that want backup protection but do not want to enlarge the pit.

    Exterior Drainage and Yard Sump Pumps in Longmont

    Sunken patios, below-grade driveways and deep window wells collect water with nowhere to go. For those areas we set an exterior basin with a submersible pump and run the discharge to a safe point, often a pop-up emitter in the yard. Slopes toward the house are the usual cause. The outlet sits well clear of the foundation and the property line.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Longmont, CO

    Storms knock out power at the exact moment the pit is filling fastest. A battery backup pump sits beside the primary and runs on its own float. Most run five to seven hours on a full charge. We mount the charger on the wall, off the floor.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Longmont, CO

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the pit, so the motor never sits in water. You can reach the motor and the switch without fishing anything out of the pit, which helps in a basement where the pit is shallow or tight. They are louder than submersibles. We set the float rod and the discharge so the pump cycles cleanly.

In clay the water stays against the foundation longer, so the pump runs more often and a larger basin helps the motor last. That changes the pump we pick.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Installation in Longmont

    Most Longmont, CO sump pump jobs land between $600 and $1,800. That covers the pump, the check valve and labor. New pits cost more. We write it all down before work starts.

    Seven to ten years is typical. Cast iron pumps with mechanical switches tend to outlast plastic ones, and a pump that rests longer between cycles lasts longer. Cleaning the pit helps.

    Most standard installs take three to six hours. A new pit can stretch it to a full day. A simple swap is quicker.

    Not every home needs one. Homes with basements, crawl spaces, sunken patios or yards that collect runoff get the most from a pump, since storm water has nowhere else to go.

    Submersibles are quieter and stronger, so they suit heavy use. A pedestal pump costs less and you can reach the motor without lifting it out, but it makes more noise and moves less water.

    Strange noises, nonstop running and visible rust are the usual signs. A quick test before the rainy season catches most problems.

    Discharge should end at least ten feet from the foundation, on ground that slopes away. Plenty of cities ban discharge into the sanitary sewer.
