    Backflow preventers stop contaminated water from flowing backward into the public supply during pressure drops. Irrigation, fire lines and commercial equipment are common sources of contamination. Water providers require them to protect public health.

    Most water providers require testing at least once per year. Some require testing after installation, repair or relocation as well. Your provider's notice will list the due date.

    New installations and replacements often require a plumbing permit and an initial test. Requirements vary by city and water provider. We obtain permits and handle the initial test.

    Signs of a failing backflow preventer include discolored water, unusual tastes or odors in your water, water leaking from the relief valve, or a sudden drop in water pressure. If you notice any of these issues, it is important to have your device inspected promptly.
