    Jet Pump System Repair in Castle Rock, CO

    Jet pumps, typically found above ground, use an ejector assembly to draw water from the well. Common problems include air leaks in the suction line, clogged nozzles, or a failing motor. These issues can lead to intermittent water flow or a pump that runs continuously without building pressure. GFR Plumbing can effectively troubleshoot and repair these components, ensuring your jet pump system in Castle Rock operates at peak efficiency.     Pressure Switch Replacement in Castle Rock

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Pitted contacts, clogged sensing tubes and insect damage are frequent causes of failure. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank. This is one of the most affordable well repairs.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Castle Rock, CO

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. That rapid cycling burns out pumps early. We check the air charge, inspect the bladder and recharge or replace the tank. Correct tank sizing protects the pump and gives steadier pressure at the tap.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Repair in Castle Rock

    Minor repairs like a pressure switch or capacitor usually cost $150 to $500. Pressure tank replacement often runs $600 to $1,500, and replacing a deep submersible pump can range from $1,800 to $5,000 or more. We quote the price before any work starts.

    Common signs include no water, inconsistent water pressure, air sputtering from faucets, strange noises coming from the well or pressure tank, or the pump running constantly. If you notice any of these, it's smart to call for a professional inspection.

    Most pump replacements take four to eight hours once parts are on site. Very deep wells or stuck pumps can take more time.

    Improving well water pressure can involve a few solutions, such as adjusting the pressure switch settings, checking for clogs in the piping, or ensuring your pressure tank is functioning correctly. In some cases, a booster pump or a larger well pump might be necessary. Our Castle Rock experts can assess your system and recommend the best course of action.

    Well pump repair involves complex electrical components, high-pressure water systems, and often heavy equipment for pulling pumps from wells. DIY repair is generally not recommended due to safety risks and the potential for further damage. It's best left to experienced professionals to ensure a correct and safe repair.

    Submersible pumps commonly last 8 to 15 years, depending on run time, sand and water quality. Short cycling from a bad pressure tank can shorten that a lot.
