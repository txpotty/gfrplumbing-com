    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Castle Rock

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Castle Rock

    Conversions in Castle Rock tend to run a full workday. We drain and remove the tank, upsize and test the gas, run a new vent through a wall or roof, then mount, plumb and fire the unit with service valves. You shower that night. Inspection follows within days.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Installation in Castle Rock

    Sizing a home unit comes down to two numbers: peak gallons per minute and temperature rise. A shower head flows about 2 gallons per minute and a kitchen faucet a bit less, so a house that runs two showers and a sink together needs roughly 5 to 6 gallons at the rise your inlet water calls for. That narrows the model list fast.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Castle Rock, CO

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Castle Rock, CO homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. A whole-home electric unit draws heavy amperage. Some need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, and a 100-amp panel often cannot carry that on top of the air conditioner and range. We check the panel first.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Installation in Castle Rock, CO

    Rinnai has been making tankless heaters for decades, and parts are easy to get. We install both condensing and non-condensing Rinnai models, and the right one depends on where the vent can go and how much you want to spend up front. The non-condensing ones need stainless vent pipe. Condensing models use PVC vent and a condensate drain.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Services in Castle Rock, CO

    Businesses in Castle Rock, CO with steady hot water use, like salons, restaurants and small gyms, often get more out of two or three tankless units racked together than one big tank. One unit can fail and the rest still carry the load. We size the rack by peak demand. Expect a bigger gas line and a mechanical permit on commercial jobs.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Installation in Castle Rock

    Navien builds condensing tankless units with stainless heat exchangers, and several models come with a recirculation pump built in. The built-in pump can move hot water toward a distant bath on a timer, cutting the wait at the tap without a separate pump. Venting is usually two-inch or three-inch PVC. Condensing units also need a condensate drain.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Castle Rock, CO

    Switching from a tank takes more than unbolting one and hanging the other. A 40-gallon tank burns about 40,000 BTU, but a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the meter line nearly always gets bigger. Venting changes too. Both are on the estimate.
