    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in Castle Rock

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. Inside, the pipe rusts once the zinc is gone. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, which is why pressure drops and the water runs orange after a shutoff, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.

    Burst Main Line Response in Castle Rock, CO

    Burst lines do damage fast. We get the water off, dig to the break, and decide on site whether a clamp or a short section of new pipe will hold until a full replacement can be scheduled, or whether the line is too far gone to bother patching. You will not be without water overnight if we can help it.

    Copper Water Line Installation in Castle Rock

    Type K copper is the thick-wall grade made for burial. It handles high street pressure without complaint. We bed it in sand, keep it away from sharp rock, and sleeve it where it passes through a foundation wall, because concrete and copper rubbing together for twenty years eventually wear a hole. PEX is cheaper.

    Emergency Water Line Repair and Replacement in Castle Rock, CO

    No water at all, or water pouring into the yard? call (720) 727-9398. We find the break, clamp or splice it if the pipe can hold, and give you a straight answer on whether the line needs a full replacement, along with a date to do it. Overnight fixes happen.

    Damage Assessment Before Replacement in Castle Rock

    We start at the meter. If the little dial on the meter is spinning with every fixture off, water is leaving somewhere. From there we listen along the line with an acoustic ground mic, check the house side for a toilet or softener running, and sometimes push a camera into the line when we suspect root damage or a crushed section, so the hole goes in the right spot.

    PEX Water Line Installation in Castle Rock, CO

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Hard freezes are a normal part of winter in Colorado, and pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, and garages are particularly vulnerable. A frozen PEX line often survives where copper would crack. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and we keep it out of direct sun at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.

    Type L vs. Type K Copper in Castle Rock, CO

    The thickest wall belongs to Type K, the usual choice underground. Type L is lighter and mostly goes indoors. Some codes allow L for burial, but we stick with K for service lines because a thicker wall buys more years against corrosive soil and stray rocks. Same fittings, different pipe.

    Copper Service Line Installation in Castle Rock, CO

    Copper is still the choice for some homeowners. It has a long track record. We flare or use brass compression fittings at the ends, keep the run in one piece between them, and pack sand around it so rocks do not touch the pipe. Where it goes through a concrete wall, it gets a plastic sleeve and a seal to keep water and bugs out.
