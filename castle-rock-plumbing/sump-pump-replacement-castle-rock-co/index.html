    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Castle Rock

    Many sump pump failures start with a stuck float switch or a leaking check valve. A failed check valve lets water flow back into the pit and causes the pump to short cycle. We install new check valves with every pump replacement and verify the switch moves without binding. Vertical float switches work well in tight pits. Tethered floats suit larger pits.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Castle Rock

    Basement bathrooms and laundry rooms below the sewer line need sewage ejector pumps instead of sump pumps. These pumps handle solids and must be installed in sealed, vented basins. We replace ejector pumps with models rated for two inch solids. We also check the basin seal, vent and discharge check valve. Proper installation prevents sewer gas odors.

    Sump Pump Basin and Pit Replacement in Castle Rock, CO

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Castle Rock

    A standard sump pump replacement usually costs $500 to $1,200 installed. Battery backup systems usually add $800 to $1,800. Sewage ejector pumps usually cost $1,200 to $2,500.

    GFR Plumbing provides warranty coverage on both the sump pump unit itself, per the manufacturer's warranty, and our workmanship. We stand behind our installations for your peace of mind and a dependable, long-lasting solution.

    Manufacturer warranties typically range from 3 to 5 years. We also offer a labor warranty.

    To maintain your new sump pump, regularly check the pit for debris, test the pump monthly by pouring water into the pit, and make sure the discharge pipe is clear. An annual professional inspection by GFR Plumbing can also help extend its lifespan.

    Most standard sump pump replacements can be completed within 2 to 4 hours. More complex installations, such as those requiring new pit excavation or major discharge line work, might take longer, up to a full day.

    A battery backup sump pump system is a secondary pump powered by a battery, designed to turn on automatically if your primary sump pump loses power during an outage or fails. This is critical for maintaining flood protection during power outages caused by severe weather.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, powered by your home's electricity. A backup sump pump, usually battery-powered or water-powered, is a secondary system that kicks on if the primary pump fails, loses power or cannot keep up with heavy water inflow. Many Castle Rock homes are better off with a backup system due to storm risks.

    Sump pumps usually have a lifespan of 7 to 10 years. Even if they appear to be working, their efficiency can decrease over time. Regular inspection and testing are crucial, and replacement is advisable as they approach the end of their expected service life.
