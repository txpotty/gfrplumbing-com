    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Centennial

    A spot repair on an accessible service line typically runs $350 to $1,500 depending on depth and access. Full replacement from meter to house usually costs $1,800 to $5,500, and trenchless replacement often falls between $3,000 and $7,000. We give you a written price before starting.

    In most communities the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. The homeowner is responsible for the service line from the meter to the house. We check which side the leak is on before quoting you.

    Many policies do not cover the pipe itself but may cover resulting damage to your home. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their utility or insurer. We provide photos and an itemized invoice to back up your claim.

    Trenchless water line repair is a method of repairing or replacing underground pipes without extensive digging. It involves creating small access points and using specialized equipment to either pull a new pipe through the old one (pipe bursting) or insert a new lining into the existing pipe (pipe lining). This method saves landscaping and reduces repair time.

    Preventing future water line issues involves a few key steps: being mindful of landscaping near buried lines, quickly fixing minor leaks, and considering a whole-home water pressure regulator if your pressure is consistently high. Regular plumbing inspections can also help identify potential problems before they grow. For older homes in Centennial, proactive pipe replacement might be the best long-term solution.

    We typically install Type K copper, PEX rated for direct burial or HDPE for trenchless pulls. Each holds up well in most soils when properly bedded. We will recommend the right material based on your soil, line length and budget.

    Common signs include a wet or unusually green patch of yard, a hissing sound near the meter, low pressure at every fixture and a water bill that climbs without explanation. Dirty or rusty water can also signal a damaged line. If your meter moves with all fixtures off, you probably have a leak.

    Common signs of a water line leak include an unexplained increase in your water bill, damp or soggy spots in your yard (even during dry weather), a noticeable drop in water pressure, discolored water, or the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use. If you notice any of these, it's wise to call a professional plumber right away.
