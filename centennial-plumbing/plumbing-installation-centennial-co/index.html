    What We Install in Centennial, CO

    Water Heater Installation in Centennial

    Tank or tankless, gas or electric, the first question is how many people shower in the morning. Sizing starts with your peak morning demand and the fuel you have, and we look at venting and gas supply before giving a number. Gas heaters need the closest look. Bad flue pitch or a spilling draft hood can send carbon monoxide into the living space. We haul the old tank away before we leave.

    Expansion Tank and Sewage Ejector Installs in Centennial, CO

    A closed water system has nowhere for heated water to expand, so pressure climbs every single time the heater cycles. The expansion tank takes up that swing so the relief valve stops weeping. A PRV usually means you need one. For basement baths and guest suites below the sewer, we also install ejector systems with a sealed basin, check valve and vent.

    Natural Gas and Propane Lines in Centennial, CO

    Natural gas and propane run at different pressures, and the pipe sizing changes with them. Our licensed plumbers size each run from the appliance BTU ratings and the developed length, then choose black iron or CSST to suit the route. CSST must be bonded. Without that bond, a lightning strike nearby can arc through the tubing and start a fire.

    PEX Pipe Installation and Repiping in Centennial

    Pinhole leaks in copper keep coming back once the pipe walls start to thin. A PEX repipe ends that cycle. The layout is a manifold with home runs for balanced pressure, or trunk and branch when the framing makes home runs a fight. We cut small, clean access holes and patch them, or hand off to your drywall contractor if you prefer.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Centennial, CO

    A basement bathroom sits below the sewer line, so gravity cannot carry the waste out. An ejector pump in a sealed basin lifts it up to the main drain. Pump and basin are sized to the fixtures, the basin is vented to code, and the discharge gets a check valve and a shutoff so the pump can come out for service. Brands we trust here are Liberty and Zoeller.

    PEX Repiping in Centennial

    Copper repipes cost more and take longer, which is why most whole-house repipes today use PEX-A or PEX-B tubing with brass or poly fittings. It runs quieter than copper, with less water hammer banging in the walls. It also handles a freeze better. Frost-free hose bibs are standard here, but they still burst if a hose is left attached when temperatures drop. We use fittings matched to the tubing brand.

    Water Line Installation in Centennial

    Galvanized steel pipe rusts from the inside, so pressure drops a little every year until the shower barely runs. We replace it with PEX or copper and make every connection on the new line leak-free before backfill. Mountain homes in Colorado frequently rely on private wells, which may need treatment for iron, manganese, or other minerals. Pipe choice follows the water. That includes the service line from the meter, the branch lines inside and the fixture supplies, all trenched and backfilled to local rules.

    Water Line Installation and Replacement in Centennial

    Corroded galvanized and brittle polybutylene are the two pipes we replace most. We run new lines in PEX or Type L copper, using crimp or expansion fittings on the PEX and soldered joints on the copper. Basements are common in Colorado, and shifting soil can crack floor drains, sewer lines, and foundation walls over time. We bed buried pipe in sand at the right depth so stones and settling soil cannot chafe it over time. Street pressure over 80 psi gets a PRV.
