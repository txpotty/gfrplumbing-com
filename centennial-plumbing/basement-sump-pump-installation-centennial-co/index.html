    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Centennial

    Prices in Centennial, CO usually land between $800 and $2,500. Pedestal pumps cost less than cast iron submersibles, and the biggest swing comes from whether we have to excavate a new pit and whether you want a battery backup added to the same basin. Estimates are itemized.

    Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps cover most of our installs. Horsepower and lift decide which model goes in, then by features like a vertical float or a built-in alarm, so the pump fits your pit, your budget and the amount of water your Centennial basement actually takes on. Cast iron is our default.

    Housing in Colorado ranges from older homes with galvanized and cast iron pipe to fast-growing suburbs built with plastic and PEX. Slab homes without a basement rarely need a pump. Houses with basements or below-grade rooms are another story, since they can take on water fast during heavy storms, especially where the ground sheds rain rather than soaking it up. A cleanup costs more than a pit.

    Discharge belongs in the yard, a dry well, or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Tying a sump pump into the sanitary sewer is not allowed in most places, and it can draw a fine.

    Permit rules vary by town, including Centennial. A straight pump replacement usually does not, but a new pit, a new outlet or a new exit through the foundation wall often does, and we sort that out before any concrete gets cut. We handle the paperwork.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Other signs are rust on the housing and rapid cycling, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. Past ten years, replace it.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. The secondary, usually battery powered, sits beside it as a backup, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Each pump gets its own check valve.

    A straight pump replacement generally goes without a permit. New pits, new discharge routes and new circuits may, so we look up the local rules and file for a permit when one is needed.
