    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Centennial

    Many sump pump failures start with a stuck float switch or a leaking check valve. A failed check valve lets water flow back into the pit and causes the pump to short cycle. We install new check valves with every pump replacement and verify the switch moves freely. Vertical float switches work well in tight pits. Tethered floats work best in larger pits.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Replacement in Centennial, CO

    Pedestal sump pumps have their motor mounted above the sump pit, making them easier to access for maintenance and often more durable. We provide professional pedestal sump pump replacement in Centennial, CO, with proper alignment and secure mounting. This type is often preferred in shallower pits or where noise is not a primary concern, offering a robust solution for flood prevention.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Centennial

    The cost of sump pump replacement in Centennial, CO typically ranges from $500 to $1,500, depending on the type of pump, submersible or pedestal, its horsepower and any needed pit changes. Battery backup systems will add to this cost, usually between $300 and $800. We provide a written, upfront quote after assessing your needs.

    To maintain your new sump pump, regularly check the pit for debris, test the pump monthly by pouring water into the pit, and make sure the discharge pipe is clear. An annual professional inspection by GFR Plumbing can also help lengthen its lifespan.

    If storms knock out power in your area or you have finished living space below grade, a backup is a wise investment. A battery unit with a high-water alarm usually runs six to ten hours of intermittent pumping on a full charge.

    Key signs include the pump not turning on, unusual noises such as grinding or rattling, running nonstop, visible rust or corrosion, or an age over 7 to 10 years. Any of these could point to coming failure and a need for replacement.

    Simple replacements typically do not require permits. New discharge lines or electrical work may, depending on your city.

    Most sump pumps last 7 to 10 years. Pumps that sit idle for long periods can fail sooner due to stuck switches.

    It is recommended to test your sump pump at least every three months, and especially before and after rainy seasons. To test, simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit. The pump should activate, drain the water and then shut off automatically. If it does not, it is time for a professional inspection.
