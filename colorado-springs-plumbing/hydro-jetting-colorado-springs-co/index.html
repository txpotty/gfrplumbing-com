    Hydro Jetting vs. Snaking Cost Comparison in Colorado Springs, CO

    While snaking might be cheaper upfront for simple clogs, hydro jetting offers a more thorough and long-lasting solution, often saving money in the long run for Colorado Springs, CO residents. Snaking usually punches a hole through a blockage, leaving residue behind, which can lead to recurring clogs. Hydro jetting cleans the entire pipe, preventing future issues and reducing the frequency of service calls, making it a more economical choice for persistent drain problems.

    Preventive Maintenance Jetting in Colorado Springs

    Kitchen Line Grease Jetting in Colorado Springs, CO

    Kitchen drains are the most common grease trouble spot in any home or restaurant. Grease clings to pipe walls and hardens, especially where the line runs flat under a slab.     Residential Sewer Line Jetting in Colorado Springs

    Main sewer lines in homes are usually 3 or 4 inches and can collect years of grease, soap, mineral scale and roots. We feed jetting hose through a cleanout and work it toward the street, cleaning in passes. Rear-facing jets pull the nozzle forward while forward jets break up heavy blockages. The water carries debris down to the city main. A camera run afterward shows the pipe walls clean and open.

    Root Cutting With Hydro Jetting in Colorado Springs

    Specialty root-cutting nozzles spin or use focused streams to slice through fine roots inside a sewer. This is effective on root hairs that a cable saw can leave behind. Roots from yard trees and shrubs often enter at clay pipe joints or cracks. Jetting clears them out and gives a clean view of each entry point on camera. That helps you decide whether lining or a spot repair is the next step.

    Hydro Jetting Before Pipe Lining in Colorado Springs, CO

    Cured-in-place pipe lining needs a clean surface to adhere and seal correctly. We jet the host pipe to remove roots, scale and debris, then camera it to confirm it is ready. Any heavy obstructions are cut out before the liner is installed. Skipping this step can lead to wrinkles or poor bonding. Proper prep is part of why lined pipe can last many years.
