    Navien Tankless Water Heater Repair in Colorado Springs

    Navien condensing units use a secondary stainless exchanger to recover heat from the flue gas. That makes condensate, and a plugged trap or a full neutralizer will shut the heater down with a drain or flue error. We clear the trap, check the PVC vent pitch and test the circulation pump on NPE-A models with built-in recirculation. Then we fire it at low and high flow.

    Condensing Tankless Repair in Colorado Springs, CO

    Condensing tankless heaters pull extra heat out of the exhaust and make acidic condensate that has to drain somewhere. That condensate runs through a trap and, where code or the manual calls for it, a neutralizer filled with limestone or marble chips before it reaches the drain. A plugged trap will lock the heater out. We clean the trap, refill the neutralizer and check the PVC vent for sag or the wrong slope.

    Tankless Descaling and Flushing in Colorado Springs

    Rinnai shows an LC code and Navien posts a scale warning when the exchanger runs hot from mineral buildup. Clear the code without flushing and it comes right back, because the scale is still in there. We connect a circulation pump to the service valves, circulate descaling solution through the exchanger for close to an hour, then flush and clean the inlet screen.     Electric Tankless Element Repair in Colorado Springs, CO

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Repair in Colorado Springs

    Descale yearly on hard water and clean the inlet screen every few months. Putting a softener or scale cartridge upstream cuts scale buildup a great deal, and keeping the intake clear of leaves and lint heads off combustion faults. Check the vent cap after storms.

    Hard water drops scale inside the heat exchanger every time it heats, and those passages are small. Skip the yearly flush and the unit runs hotter, loses output at high flow and eventually locks out on an overheat or scale code, which turns a routine visit into a heat exchanger replacement. The flush takes about an hour.

    You can clean the inlet water filter and the air intake screen on most models with the manual open beside you. Gas valves, igniters and anything behind the sealed combustion cover should go to a licensed plumber, and DIY work there can void the manufacturer warranty. Leave the burner alone.

    Watch for a longer wait for hot water than you are used to, a burner that cycles on and off, water around the base, or a code on the display. A drip inside the cabinet rusts parts fast.

    Low flow at a single fixture, a dirty inlet filter, a failing flow sensor or scale can all cause swings. A crossed hot and cold line at a single-handle faucet can do it too, which is a fixture problem, not a heater problem.

    Cleaning the inlet filter and wiping down the intake screen are fair homeowner jobs.
