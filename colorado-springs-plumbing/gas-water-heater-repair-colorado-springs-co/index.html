    All Major Gas Water Heater Brands Serviced in Colorado Springs

    Every brand label we see in garages and closets uses one of a handful of gas control designs. Rheem and Ruud share most parts, and State shares a lot with A.O. Smith. Bradford White runs its own control system, so we stock those separately. Knowing which family a heater belongs to lets the tech bring the right valve, thermocouple or igniter on the first trip instead of ordering parts and coming back two days later.     Propane Water Heater Repair in Colorado Springs

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. Propane units need a matching orifice and a control set near 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. Wrong parts get swapped for the right ones.

    Thermostat and Gas Valve Repair in Colorado Springs, CO

    Scalding water at the kitchen tap is a valve problem until proven otherwise. Some controls drift high as the sensor ages, and some dials get turned past hot. Our plumbers in Colorado Springs, CO measure the outlet temperature at the nearest fixture, compare it to the dial and replace the gas control if it is overshooting, because water at 140 degrees can burn a child's skin in a few seconds. A setting near 120 degrees works for most households.

    Flammable Vapor Sensor Repair in Colorado Springs

    Since about 2003, most residential gas tanks have been built with a flammable vapor ignition resistant combustion chamber. Air enters through a flame arrestor screen at the base that also keeps flame inside. When lint, dust or pet hair coats that screen, the burner starves, the chamber overheats and a thermal switch shuts the whole heater down, sometimes several times a week. We clean the screen and the chamber. We replace any tripped thermal switch.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Colorado Springs, CO

    Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves report faults with a blinking LED, and the flash count matches a chart on the label. The tech reads the code and tests the thermopile and sensors before condemning the valve. Valves do fail. When the control itself is the problem, we replace it with an exact-match valve or a listed universal model, set the temperature and leak test every joint on the gas train before relighting. We leave the old valve with you if you ask.

    Venting and Draft Repair in Colorado Springs, CO

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot. A loose, rusted or sagging section lets exhaust spill back into the house. The vent gets repaired. Each joint gets three screws, the connector gets proper clearance from wood and drywall, and we run the heater for several minutes with a smoke test at the draft hood to watch the exhaust go up. We do not leave with the heater spilling.

    Tank Leaks and Anode Rods in Colorado Springs

    Corrosion starts at the anode rod and works its way into the steel once the rod is spent. Many larger systems in Colorado draw on mountain snowmelt that runs fairly soft, while plains communities and groundwater sources often deliver more mineral-heavy water. The tech pulls the anode, checks what metal remains and swaps it if bare core wire shows. A fresh anode, a drained tank and a relief valve that seats properly can add years to a heater in a Colorado Springs home that has not started leaking from the shell. Water from the shell seam is another story. That tank needs replacing.
