    Full Home PEX Repiping in Colorado Springs

    If your home has old, corroded or leaking pipes, a full home PEX repipe can drastically improve your plumbing system. We remove outdated galvanized, copper or polybutylene pipes and replace them with durable PEX, upgrading your entire water delivery system. This prevents future leaks, improves water pressure and eliminates the rust-colored water often seen with older piping in Colorado Springs.

    Manifold and Home-Run PEX Layouts in Colorado Springs, CO

    A manifold system sends a dedicated line from a central hub to each fixture, much like a breaker panel for water. Each line gets its own shutoff, which makes repairs easier and keeps pressure steady when multiple fixtures run. Home-run layouts use more pipe but fewer fittings in walls. We often combine manifold and trunk-and-branch designs to balance cost and performance.

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Colorado Springs, CO

    PEX-A, such as Uponor, uses an expansion tool to stretch the pipe and a ring over the fitting, which then shrinks back into a secure seal. The fittings have a larger inner diameter, so flow restriction is lower. PEX-A is also more flexible and can be repaired from kinks with a heat gun. It is our preferred choice for many full repipes.

    PEX Main Water Line Installation in Colorado Springs

    The main water line brings water into your home, and a failing one can cause major issues. We provide professional PEX main water line installation, replacing old, damaged lines with durable PEX. This upgrade improves water flow, reduces the risk of future leaks and offers strong resistance to ground shifting and corrosion. Our team handles proper trenching and the connection to your municipal supply.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Colorado Springs, CO

    Gray polybutylene pipe was common in homes built from the late 1970s through the mid 1990s and is known for sudden failures. Chlorine in municipal water breaks down the plastic and its fittings over time. Plenty of insurers now ask about polybutylene when writing a policy. Replacing it with PEX removes that risk and can make your home easier to sell and insure.
