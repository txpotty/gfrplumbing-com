    Toilet Installation Jobs We Handle in Colorado Springs, CO

    Rough-In Toilet Installation in Colorado Springs, CO

    Rough-in is the plumbing that goes in before walls and floors close up. On a toilet, that is a vented 3 inch drain and a cold water stub. We set the drain 12 inches from the framed wall plus the thickness of drywall, so the finished rough-in lands right where the fixture expects it. Then the inspector sees it open.

    New Construction Toilet Installation in Colorado Springs

    On new builds we set toilets after the finish floor is down, never before. The flange should sit on top of the finished floor. Set it too early and the tile setter has to cut around it, which leaves the flange low and forces a thick seal or an extender later on. Each bowl gets a fresh seal.

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Colorado Springs, CO

    High-efficiency toilets flush on 1.28 gallons or less, against 3.5 gallons or more for toilets from before the early 1990s. Check for the WaterSense label. A MaP rating of 800 grams or better is what we look for, since a weak low-flow toilet that needs double flushing saves no water at all. Dual-flush models add a lighter half flush.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Installation in Colorado Springs, CO

    Wall-hung toilets need a wall at least 2 by 6 deep, or a furred-out chase built in front of an existing 2 by 4 wall. The floor underneath stays open. Cleaning gets easier, and the bowl height can be set anywhere within the carrier range, useful for a tall family. Service happens through the flush plate.

    Standard Toilet Installation in Colorado Springs

    A standard floor-mount toilet is a two-hour job when the flange is sound. The stop gets shut, the tank drained, and the old bowl lifted out. After scraping the old wax off, we check the flange for cracks, set new stainless bolts, and lower the new toilet straight down onto a fresh seal so it compresses evenly. The old one leaves with us.

    Toilet Brands We Install in Colorado Springs

    We install Kohler, TOTO, American Standard, Gerber and most other brands sold at supply houses and home centers. Every brand has its quirks. Some skirted TOTO models use a Unifit adapter, and certain Kohler tanks take a gasket that hardware stores rarely stock. We keep those parts on hand.

    Low-Flow WaterSense Toilet Installation in Colorado Springs, CO

    WaterSense toilets are capped at 1.28 gallons per flush, and some dual-flush models drop to around 0.8 for liquid waste. That adds up fast. Replacing a single 3.5 gallon toilet in a household of four can save several thousand gallons a year, and some water utilities have offered rebates on high-efficiency models, so it is worth asking yours. Pick a strong MaP rating.

    Commercial Toilet Installation in Colorado Springs, CO

    Commercial restrooms take a beating, so we install vitreous china bowls with flushometer valves rather than tank toilets. A flushometer needs a 1 inch supply and steady pressure. We confirm the building line can deliver the valve's rated flow, often 20 to 25 gallons a minute, before any fixture goes up. Fixture counts follow code.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Colorado Springs

    Most homeowners in Colorado Springs, CO pay $200 to $500 to have a toilet installed, fixture not included. A stubborn old toilet adds time. Bolts rusted solid have to be cut with a hacksaw or oscillating tool, and a cast iron flange that breaks during removal needs a repair ring, each of which adds labor.

    It depends on the floor and the flange. A straight swap on a solid flange is a reasonable DIY job. Hire out new tile jobs, broken flanges, wall-hung carriers and relocations, since mistakes there hide under the floor.

    Plenty of handy homeowners do. What goes wrong most is cranked-down bolts or a bowl that rocks. Overtightening cracks the porcelain, sometimes days later, and a rocking bowl breaks the wax seal so water seeps into the subfloor where you will not see it until the floor goes soft. On new floors or wall-hung units, hire it out.

    Watch the base and the tank. Water that reappears on the floor after you wipe it up points to a cracked bowl or a failed seal. A seal can be replaced for far less than a new toilet, so we check that first, but a crack in the china means replacement, since there is no lasting repair for cracked porcelain.

    Adding or moving a toilet almost always needs one. Swapping in the same place usually does not. The permit covers drain sizing and venting, and the inspector wants to see both before the floor or wall is closed, so we schedule the inspection around your remodel timeline.

    A one-piece toilet is cast as a single unit, so there is no tank-to-bowl joint to leak. Expect a higher price and more weight. Two-piece toilets join tank and bowl with a gasket, so they are easier to carry upstairs and you can replace one half if it cracks. Both flush well.

    Both seal fine on a flange set at the right height. Wax is cheap and proven. Wax-free foam and rubber seals survive being lifted and reset, and they hold up better over heated floors. We pick based on your flange height.

    Lower water bills come first. An old 3.5 gallon toilet uses almost three times the water of a 1.28 gallon model. Modern bowls clog less thanks to wider glazed trapways, and the smoother porcelain is easier to keep clean.
