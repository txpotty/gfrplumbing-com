    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Colorado Springs

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. The backup is a common add.

    Quality cast iron pumps usually carry three to five years of factory coverage. Our labor has its own warranty on top of that, and we walk you through the exact terms for the model you pick before we install it.

    Permit rules vary by town, including Colorado Springs. Replacing a pump in the same pit rarely needs one, but a new pit, a new outlet or a new exit through the foundation wall often does, and we sort that out before any concrete gets cut. The paperwork is on us.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. What decides it is how fast the pit fills and how high the water rises, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Bigger is not always better.

    Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps cover most of our installs. We pick the model by horsepower and lift, then by features like a vertical float or a built-in alarm, so the pump fits your pit, your budget and the amount of water your Colorado Springs basement actually takes on. We default to cast iron.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and a concrete patch takes most of a day, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Plan on 4 to 8 hours for a standard install. Digging a new pit and patching the slab can take the whole day, and tricky electrical access or a long discharge run through a finished wall adds time on top of that. You get a time estimate first.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. Rust stains and a float that sticks are warning signs too, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Replace it before the next storm.
