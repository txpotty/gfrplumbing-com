    What Goes Into an Ejector System in Colorado Springs, CO

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Colorado Springs

    Residential ejector systems in Colorado Springs serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. The setup is a sealed basin, a pump, a float switch, a check valve and a vent. The parts are simple. Most problems come from the install, not the hardware, so we take our time on the vent and the lid seal.

    Grinder Pump Installation in Colorado Springs

    Grinder pumps chop waste into a fine slurry first, which lets them push through small pipe over long distances. A grinder makes sense when the sewer is distant, uphill or pressurized, such as a pressure sewer where each home pumps into a shared main. Grinders run pricier. A standard ejector often covers a basement bath.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Colorado Springs

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps seem similar at first but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. They are not interchangeable.

    Liberty Pumps Installation in Colorado Springs

    Liberty ejector pumps show up on a lot of our installs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models. Their packaged systems come with the basin, lid, pump and check valve matched together, which cuts down on field fitting and gives the inspector a clean, listed assembly to look at. We still add a shutoff.

    Zoeller Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Colorado Springs

    Zoeller's sewage line runs from light residential to heavy duty, in cast iron with tethered or vertical float switches. We pick the model by horsepower and pump curve for your lift, and a typical basement bathroom lands on a four-tenths or half horsepower unit with two-inch solids handling. Tall lifts need bigger pumps.

    Ejector Pump Installation Cost Comparison in Colorado Springs, CO

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin costs the least, often done in a few hours. A new system with a basin, slab excavation, venting through the roof, electrical work and an alarm costs more, but it is the only way to add a bathroom below the sewer line in most homes without a macerating unit. Ask for both numbers.

    Upflush and Macerating Toilet Systems in Colorado Springs

    Macerating systems like Saniflo let you add a toilet and sink without breaking the concrete floor. The unit sits behind the toilet, grinds the waste and pumps it through a small discharge line, often three-quarter inch or one inch, up to the nearest drain, which makes it a good fit for a small basement half bath. They need a vent too.

    Cost-Effective Sewage Ejector Pump Upgrades in Colorado Springs, CO

    Upgrading an old ejector does not always mean a new basin. With a sound basin, a new pump, fresh check valve, shutoff and alarm give you most of a new system for far less. Cracked basins get replaced.
