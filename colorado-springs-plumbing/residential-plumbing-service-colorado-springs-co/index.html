    Water Softener and Filter Service in Colorado Springs

    Mountain homes in Colorado frequently rely on private wells, which may need treatment for iron, manganese, or other minerals.     Polybutylene, the gray plastic pipe used for years in home construction, fails from the inside without warning.     Frequently Asked Questions About Residential Plumbing Service in Colorado Springs

    Small repairs in Colorado Springs typically cost $150 to $300. A main line clog or a new water heater falls more in the $400 to $1,500 range, sometimes higher. We quote after we look. You get it in writing, and it only changes if you approve.

    Watch for drips that will not stop, slow drains, banging or whistling pipes, a sudden drop in pressure, discolored water and wet spots with no obvious source. Each one is a symptom of something that will get worse. Do not wait on them.

    Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. Soft water has had most of those minerals removed, usually by an ion-exchange softener, and the difference shows up as scale in water heaters, white crust on faucets, spotty dishes and soap that will not lather well. Fixtures last longer on soft water.

    Once a year is a good rule. In homes with hard water, sediment builds faster and the tank may need flushing twice a year, and a yearly check lets us test the relief valve, look at the anode rod and catch a weeping fitting before it rusts through the jacket. Anodes usually last three to five years.

    Many do, especially anything that changes the system. New installations and gas line work generally need a permit from the Colorado Springs building department, and we know which jobs trigger one and which do not. We file the paperwork for you.

    Our plumbers install and service Moen, Delta, Kohler, American Standard, TOTO, Rheem, Bradford White and Navien, among others. We can point you to the models that hold up best with your water. A few cost more up front and save on repairs later.

    Some jobs are fine to DIY. A flapper swap or a trap cleanout is a reasonable weekend task, but gas lines, water heaters and anything inside a wall can turn into a flood, a fire risk or a failed inspection if the work is off. A licensed plumber gets those right and to code.

    Tightening a loose connection or plunging a toilet is fine. We usually advise against complex repairs on your own, because a bad sweat joint, a cross-threaded fitting or a gas connection done wrong can cost far more than the original repair would have. Gas work in particular should go to a licensed plumber.
