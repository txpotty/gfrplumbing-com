    Well Pump & Pressure Tank Replacement in Boulder

    Rural homes around Boulder that run on a well go dry the moment the pump stops. The old pump comes up, wire and drop pipe get checked, and the new pump goes in at the right depth, often with a new tank and switch too. Most jobs finish same day.

    Water Heater Replacement in Boulder

    Water heaters in Boulder that leak from the tank, rumble on every heat cycle or run out of hot water halfway through a shower are usually cheaper to replace than to keep repairing. A leak from the tank body itself cannot be fixed. We match the fuel and size, update the venting and add an expansion tank or drain pan if code calls for one. Most swaps take a few hours.

    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Boulder

    A well pump that clicks on and off every few seconds usually points to a failed pressure tank. When the bladder inside the tank ruptures, the tank fills with water, loses its air cushion and forces the pump to start every time someone opens a tap, which burns out pump motors years early. A new tank fixes it. We also check the switch.

    Pipe Replacement & Whole-Home Repiping in Boulder, CO

    Homes in Boulder, CO with galvanized steel or polybutylene tend to show the same symptoms: low pressure, rusty water and leaks that keep coming back. A whole-home repipe replaces every supply line from the main shutoff to each fixture with PEX or copper, and in most homes it takes two to four days with water back on each evening whenever possible. Walls get patched after.

    Pipe Replacement and Repiping in Boulder

    Corroded, leaking or galvanized pipe in Boulder can stain water and cause costly damage. Failing sections or the whole house get new PEX or copper, routed to keep openings small and pressure tested long enough to catch any slow drip. Patching is included.

    Well Pump and Pressure Tank Replacement in Boulder, CO

    Rural properties near Boulder, CO need the well pump for all their water, so a failure is an emergency. Common signs include pressure that rises and falls, sputtering faucets, a pump that runs constantly or a breaker that keeps tripping, and any of those means the pump, switch or tank is close to giving out. Pumps rarely recover once this starts.
