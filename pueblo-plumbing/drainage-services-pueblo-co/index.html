    Frequently Asked Questions About Drainage Services in Pueblo

    Cost depends on whether it is a clog or a construction job. Clearing a fixture or main line is a few hundred dollars at most. Jetting and sewer repairs cost more, and yard drains are priced by length and digging. We quote in writing first.

    Clearing a clog does not need a permit. Repairs to the pipe itself often do. Replacing a section of sewer, adding a cleanout, or connecting a new drain to the sewer generally requires a permit and an inspection, and we handle that paperwork and schedule the inspector as part of the job.

    Drain cleaning usually means a cable machine with a cutting head that bores through the clog. It is quick and cheap. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the whole pipe wall clean of grease, scale and roots, so it lasts longer but costs more and needs a pipe that is in good enough shape to take the pressure.

    Simple downspout extensions are a reasonable DIY project. French drains are harder. A French drain or dry well needs correct slope, the right fabric and stone, and an outlet that will not dump water on a neighbor, and digging without an 811 locate risks hitting a gas or power line.

    Our cable machines come from Spartan Tool and RIDGID, and our cameras are RIDGID SeeSnake units with built-in locators. We run cart and trailer jetters. Good equipment matters because a camera with a locator tells us exactly where and how deep a problem is, which keeps a sewer repair to one small excavation instead of a long trench.

    Drain clearing carries a service guarantee for a set period. If the line clogs again in that window, we come back. If the camera shows broken or root-filled pipe, we tell you the clearing is temporary.

    Most homes never need scheduled drain cleaning. A slow drain is the signal. Root-prone sewers and grease-heavy kitchens benefit from cleaning every one to two years.
