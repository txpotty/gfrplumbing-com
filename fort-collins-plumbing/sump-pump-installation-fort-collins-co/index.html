    Combination Sump Pump Systems in Fort Collins

    Combination units pair a primary pump and a battery backup on one pre-plumbed discharge assembly. Because everything comes assembled on a single riser, they fit into an 18-inch pit that would be too tight for two separate pumps and two check valves. That saves some labor. They suit homes that want backup protection but do not want to enlarge the pit.

    New Sump Pit Installation in Fort Collins

    No pit yet? We saw-cut the slab at the low corner, break out the concrete and dig down through soil, hardpan or rock to set an 18 by 22 inch basin. Washed gravel goes around the basin so water can reach it. Then we patch the slab. Last come the pump, the lid and the discharge line.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Fort Collins, CO

    Backup systems we install use sealed AGM batteries that do not need water. A smart charger keeps the battery topped off and sounds an alarm if the battery weakens, the primary fails or the water rises past the backup float. Batteries need replacing every few years. Finished basements get the most out of them.

    High Water Alarms and Smart Monitoring in Fort Collins

    A high water alarm is a float switch that sounds when the pit rises past normal. Wi-Fi models send a text too. Rentals need these. We add them to new installs and to pumps already in place.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Fort Collins, CO

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the pit, so the motor never sits in water. That makes service easy. They are louder than submersibles. We set the float rod and the discharge so the pump cycles cleanly.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Fort Collins

    Submersible pumps live at the bottom of the pit with a sealed, oil-cooled motor. They run quieter and move more water. The pit lid sits flush. We seal the cord grommet and the lid on every install.

    Combination Sump Pump System Installation in Fort Collins, CO

    Two pumps on one riser cover both kinds of failure, a dead motor and a dead outlet. The primary handles daily water. Finished basements are the usual reason. They are worth it anywhere a few inches of water would mean new drywall.

    Emergency Sump Pump Replacement in Fort Collins, CO

    A dead pump during a storm means the water is already climbing. Our trucks carry 1/3 and 1/2 HP submersibles, check valves and fittings, so in most homes we can pull the old pump and set a new one in one visit. Figure on two hours for a typical swap. We test it with a full pit before leaving.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Installation in Fort Collins

    Sump pump installation in Fort Collins, CO runs about $800 to $2,500 for most homes. The pump type and whether we dig a new pit move the price most. Backups add to it. We quote it in writing after a site visit.

    Grinding, rattling or a pump that runs with no water in the pit are all warning signs. Visible rust, a float that sticks or a pump that cycles every minute during dry weather usually means the end is near. If the basement flooded anyway, it is time.

    Submersibles are quieter and stronger, so they suit heavy use. A pedestal pump costs less and you can reach the motor without lifting it out, but it makes more noise and moves less water.

    Manufacturer warranties usually run three to five years, longer on premium models. We also back our workmanship.

    Discharge should end at least ten feet from the foundation, on ground that slopes away. Plenty of cities ban discharge into the sanitary sewer.

    Seven to ten years is typical. Cast iron pumps with mechanical switches tend to outlast plastic ones, and a pump that rests longer between cycles lasts longer. Cleaning the pit helps.

    Strange noises, nonstop running and visible rust are the usual signs. A quick test before the rainy season catches most problems.

    Not every home needs one. Homes with basements, crawl spaces, sunken patios or yards that collect runoff get the most from a pump, since storm water has nowhere else to go.
