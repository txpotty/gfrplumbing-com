    Gas and Electric Tank Checks in Fort Collins, CO

    Gas and electric tanks share the same steel shell, but the parts that wear out are different. Gas units get a burner, thermocouple and vent check. Electric units get a meter test on both elements and a look at the wiring at each thermostat, where loose terminals can scorch and melt the insulation. Both get a flush.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Fort Collins, CO

    Water chemistry changes from one street to the next, and so does anode life. Most rods last three to five years. A softened water supply can chew through a magnesium rod in two. A new rod costs a fraction of a tank and often adds years.

    Emergency Tank Troubleshooting in Fort Collins

    No hot water at all is usually a pilot, a gas valve, a tripped high-limit or a dead element. Most of those are quick fixes. With a leak, we first check whether water comes from a top fitting or from under the jacket. Leaks from under the jacket mean the tank itself has failed.

    Attic and Garage Tank Checkups in Fort Collins, CO

    Garage and attic tanks live through bigger temperature swings than closet units, which wears out plastic drain valves and wiring insulation faster. A pan under an attic tank is not optional. We check that the pan drain line runs to a safe spot and is not cracked or plugged, because one slow leak over a bedroom can bring down a ceiling. Gas tanks in garages need their ignition source at least 18 inches off the floor unless they are FVIR models.

    Commercial Tank Maintenance in Fort Collins, CO

    Restaurants, salons, laundromats and small offices run tanks much harder than a household does, often 75 to 100 gallons cycling all day. Scale piles up much faster than at home. We usually set commercial tanks on a quarterly or twice-yearly flush, with a dated log taped to the jacket that your insurance inspector can read. Downtime gets scheduled outside business hours.

    Gas Venting Tune-Up in Fort Collins, CO

    Atmospheric gas tanks vent by draft alone, so the vent connector needs upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot and tight joints with three screws each. We check draft at the hood. Lint on the flame arrestor of an FVIR tank can starve the burner and kill the pilot. We vacuum it clean.

    Expansion Tank Service in Fort Collins, CO

    A thermal expansion tank is the small steel tank on the cold line above the heater, and it gives heated water somewhere to go when a check valve or pressure reducer closes the system. We test the air side with a tire gauge. The precharge needs to match static pressure in the house. A waterlogged expansion tank sounds solid when you knock on it, and it lets pressure spike every time the burner fires.

    Burner and Pilot Service in Fort Collins, CO

    On a gas tank, a dirty burner throws a lazy yellow flame and leaves soot on the flue baffle. We pull the burner assembly and brush it clean. We also check the thermocouple or flame sensor, since a weak one lets the pilot go out every few days and leaves you resetting it on your knees. The goal is a steady blue flame.
