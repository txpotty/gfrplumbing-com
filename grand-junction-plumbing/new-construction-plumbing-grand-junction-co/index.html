    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Grand Junction

    Bathroom count drives the cost more than square footage does. Basement homes can cost more when fixtures sit below the sewer and need an ejector pump. Gas piping is priced separately. Ask us for a number after we see your plans, since square-foot estimates miss too much to be useful.

    Dry air, intense sun, and big daily temperature swings put constant stress on pipes, fittings, and outdoor plumbing in Colorado. Frost-free hose bibs are standard here, but they still crack if a hose is left attached when temperatures drop. Pipe routes and insulation get chosen to match. That can mean keeping supply lines out of exterior walls, insulating attic runs, and setting frost-free hose bibs that drain back inside the heated space. Attics get the most attention.

    Some jurisdictions let owner-builders plumb a home they will live in. Permits and inspections still apply. Inspectors apply the same code, and a failed underground can cost more in delay than hiring it out. Many places require a license for gas.

    Rough-in on a typical house takes one to two weeks of crew time, spread over the build. Underground runs a day or two before the pour. Top-out happens after framing and takes three to five days, then trim waits until cabinets and counters are in, which can be months later on a custom home. Trim is one or two days.

    PEX handles most water supply in new homes. It flexes and needs fewer fittings in the walls than copper. Drains and vents are usually Schedule 40 PVC or ABS, depending on what the local code allows and what the plans specify for the building. We still use copper near water heaters.

    We handle the plumbing permit work and inspections on our scope. Your builder usually pulls the main building permit. We book every plumbing inspection, meet the inspector, and clear corrections before the next trade needs the wall.

    Workmanship is covered under our written warranty, and the length is spelled out in your contract. Fixtures carry their own manufacturer warranties. Water heaters and PEX systems often carry 10 years or more, but only when installed with the matching tools and fittings, which is one reason we do not mix brands.

    Expansive clay soils are a well-known problem in parts of Colorado, swelling with moisture and lifting slabs, basement floors, and buried pipes. Ground movement puts stress on pipes under a slab. Our crews bed drains in sand or gravel, sleeve pipes through the slab and run PEX water services where permitted. Sleeves are cheap.
