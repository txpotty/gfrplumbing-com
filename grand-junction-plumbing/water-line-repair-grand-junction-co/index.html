    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Grand Junction

    The cost of water line repair in Grand Junction, CO can vary widely, typically ranging from $500 for a minor leak repair to $5,000 or more for a full main line replacement or complex trenchless work. Factors include the location of the leak, the type of pipe, the extent of damage, and whether excavation is required. We provide a clear, upfront estimate after a thorough inspection.

    Common signs of a broken water line include surprisingly high water bills, a sudden drop in water pressure, wet spots in your yard or on pavement that don't dry, discolored water, or the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use. For homes on slab foundations, watch for cracks in the foundation or warm spots on the floor.

    Many policies do not cover the pipe itself but may cover resulting damage to your home. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their utility or insurer. We provide photos and an itemized invoice to support your claim.

    In most communities the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. The homeowner is in charge of the service line from the meter to the house. We check which side the leak is on before quoting you.

    Most standard water line repairs can be completed within a few hours to a full day, depending on the severity and location of the leak. More complex issues, like trenchless pipe replacement or extensive excavation for a main line, might take 1-3 days. We always aim for the quickest, most efficient fix without compromising quality.

    While some very minor, accessible leaks might seem DIY-friendly, we strongly advise against attempting water line repairs yourself, especially for underground or main lines. Improper repairs can lead to greater damage, code violations, and costly future problems. Professional plumbers have the specialized tools, experience, and knowledge of Colorado plumbing codes to deliver a safe and lasting repair.

    Full service line replacement usually requires a plumbing permit and inspection from your city or county. Small spot repairs often do not. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when one is required.

    A homeowner can sometimes tighten a leaking fitting at an exposed hose bib. Buried service line repairs require locating, safe excavation, 811 utility marking and code-approved fittings. Mistakes can cause bigger leaks, so most homeowners call a licensed plumber.
