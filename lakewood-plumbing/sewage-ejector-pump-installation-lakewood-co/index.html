    What Goes Into an Ejector System in Lakewood, CO

    Liberty Pumps Installation in Lakewood

    Liberty ejector pumps show up on a lot of our installs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models. Their packaged systems come with the basin, lid, pump and check valve matched together, which cuts down on field fitting and gives the inspector a clean, listed assembly to look at. We still add a shutoff.

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Lakewood

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so every fixture drains to a sealed pit instead of flowing by gravity. We cut the slab, trench the drain lines to the basin, set it, run a two-inch vent up through the house to the roof and pipe the discharge to the building drain with a check valve and shutoff. The slab gets repoured.

    Emergency Ejector Pump Replacement in Lakewood, CO

    A dead ejector pump takes every below-grade fixture out of service until it is fixed. Stop using those fixtures, including the washer if it drains to the pit, then call (720) 727-9398 and we will try to get a plumber and a replacement pump to you that day. Most swaps take a few hours.

    Duplex and Commercial Ejector Systems in Lakewood, CO

    Commercial buildings, multi-unit properties and busy homes often need duplex systems with an alternating control panel. Alternating the lead pump spreads wear, and when one fails or flow spikes, the second starts on its own and the panel sounds an alarm. Redundancy matters here.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in Lakewood

    Digging the pit means cutting the slab and excavating below it, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Basements are common in Colorado, and shifting soil can crack floor drains, sewer lines, and basement walls over time. The basin sits on gravel with backfill around it so rising groundwater cannot float it, and new concrete seals the floor and locks it down. A floating basin breaks pipe.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Lakewood

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps seem similar at first but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. Never mix the two.

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Lakewood

    Residential ejector systems in Lakewood serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. The setup is a sealed basin, a pump, a float switch, a check valve and a vent. Simple parts, exact install. Install mistakes cause more trouble than hardware, so the vent and lid seal get extra attention.

    Upflush and Macerating Toilet Systems in Lakewood

    Macerating systems like Saniflo let you add a toilet and sink without breaking the concrete floor. It sits behind the toilet, grinds waste and pumps it through a small line, often three-quarter or one inch, to a drain above, which suits a small half bath. Venting still applies.
