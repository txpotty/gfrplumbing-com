    A sewer odor usually means a dry or improperly installed trap, or a venting problem. We check the trap seal and vent when we replace any drain.

    Common materials for sink drains include PVC (polyvinyl chloride) and ABS (acrylonitrile butadiene styrene) plastic, which are durable and corrosion-resistant. For visible components like pop-up assemblies, you might find chrome-plated brass or stainless steel. We'll discuss the best material options for your particular sink and usage.

    GFR Plumbing installs a variety of high-quality sink drain components from trusted brands like Moen, Delta, Kohler, and Oatey.     Common signs include persistent leaks under the sink, visible corrosion or rust on drain components, frequent clogs that return quickly after clearing, slow drainage, or a persistent foul odor coming from the drain that isn't resolved by cleaning. If you notice any of these, it is wise to have your drain inspected.

    Mountain homes in Colorado frequently rely on private wells, which may need treatment for iron, manganese, or natural minerals. Hard water can contribute to mineral buildup inside drain pipes over time, which reduces the internal diameter of the drain and leads to slower drainage and a higher risk of clogs. While it doesn't directly cause a drain to fail, it can worsen existing issues and make replacement more effective than repeated cleaning.

    PVC is affordable, corrosion-proof and a great fit inside a vanity. Brass looks better on exposed pedestal or wall-mount sinks but costs more.
