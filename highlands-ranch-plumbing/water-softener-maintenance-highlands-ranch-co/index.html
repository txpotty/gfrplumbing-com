    Softener Brands We Work On in Highlands Ranch

    Most softeners sold in the last 20 years run on a handful of valve platforms, mainly Fleck, Clack, Autotrol and a few proprietary heads. The valve matters more than the brand. Kinetico and Culligan build their own valves, so we check part availability before quoting.

    Control Valve and Timer Diagnostics in Highlands Ranch, CO

    The control valve runs every stage of regeneration, from backwash through brine draw to the final refill. We step it through each cycle by hand and watch for a motor that stalls, a drive gear that skips, a piston that drags or a drain that never shuts off. Worn pistons turn up a lot.

    Control Valve and Injector Cleaning in Highlands Ranch, CO

    Brine draw depends on a venturi injector, a tiny nozzle and throat that pulls brine out of the salt tank as water rushes past. A few specks of grit can stop it cold. We pull the injector and screen, clean them, and replace the seals and spacers on valves like the Fleck 5600 and Clack WS1 when they show wear.

    Salt-Free Conditioner Service in Highlands Ranch

    Salt-free conditioners, usually template assisted crystallization units, do not remove hardness. A hardness test still reads high afterward. They turn the hardness into microscopic crystals that mostly pass through without sticking. Plan on new media every three to five years, and the sediment prefilter ahead of it needs changing every few months, which is the part most owners skip.

    Brine Tank Cleanout and Inspection in Highlands Ranch, CO

    Brine tanks collect a gray sludge of salt fines and insoluble grit, and it builds up faster with cheap rock salt. We empty, scrub and rinse the tank. Then we look for cracks around the brine well and overflow fitting and refill with fresh salt.

    Regeneration Programming and Hardness Testing in Highlands Ranch, CO

    Plenty of softeners still run factory settings. Either the installer left the defaults in, or the family got bigger and the settings stayed put. We enter your raw hardness and household size, then set a metered valve to regenerate by gallons used rather than on a fixed timer schedule.

    Breaking Up Salt Bridges in Highlands Ranch

    To check for a bridge, we push a broom handle straight down through the salt. A hard stop, then a drop, means a bridge. We break the crust, vacuum out the salt mush below it, then check the brine well, safety float, brine line and grid plate before refilling to about half full.

    Resin Rebedding in Highlands Ranch

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water slowly swell and soften resin beads, and iron or sediment can coat them until capacity drops off. We try a resin cleaner first. If the bed is spent, we drain the tank, pull the old resin and refill it with fresh high-capacity resin, which runs far less than a new softener.
