    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Highlands Ranch

    Figure on roughly $800 to $2,000 for most basement sump pump installs in Highlands Ranch. The low end covers a pump swap in an existing pit, while a new pit cut through the slab, a long discharge run, or a battery backup pushes the total toward the top of that range. You get a written price after we look.

    Spring snowmelt raises groundwater in Colorado, which can push water into basements and strain sump pumps. Outages and storms arrive together, so we recommend a battery backup in most basements. The backup starts on its own if the main pump has no power, and it also catches the water if the primary switch sticks or the pump simply cannot keep up with a heavy inflow. Think of it as cheap insurance.

    Colorado adopts a statewide plumbing code based on the International Plumbing Code, and plumbers must hold a state license. New pits, new circuits and new discharge routes are the parts most likely to need a plumbing permit. The local requirements get checked before we start, and when a permit applies we pull it and schedule the inspection ourselves. You do not deal with the office.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. The secondary, usually battery powered, sits beside it as a backup, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Two pumps, two check valves.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. What decides it is how fast the pit fills and how high the water rises, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Oversizing causes short cycling.

    For some homeowners, a same-model pump swap is a fair weekend job. New pits and backup systems are a different matter, because those mistakes end in flooding, cracked slabs or electrical hazards, and fixing them costs more than paying for the install up front. Leave those to us.

    Permit rules vary by town, including Highlands Ranch. A straight pump replacement usually does not, but a new pit, a new outlet or a new exit through the foundation wall often does, and we sort that out before any concrete gets cut. The paperwork is on us.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. Rust stains and a float that sticks are warning signs too, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Replace it before the next storm.
