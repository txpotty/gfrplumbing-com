    Maintenance Versus Replacement Cost in Highlands Ranch, CO

    Pit Cleaning and Liner Checks in Highlands Ranch

    Long dry stretches let dust, sand, leaves and gravel settle at the bottom of the basin. Sooner or later the pump sucks it into the screen and locks up the impeller. We pump the basin dry, rinse the screen and check the plastic liner for cracks, since a cracked liner lets soil wash in from the sides. Takes about twenty minutes.

    Commercial Sump Pump Maintenance in Highlands Ranch, CO

    Commercial pits are often bigger and deeper, and many have two pumps on an alternating controller. Each pump gets tested, along with the alternator and the high-water alarm. You get a written record of each reading, which comes in handy for insurance renewals and inspections. Quarterly visits are common.

    Float Switch Checks in Highlands Ranch, CO

    The float switch tells the pump when to start and stop, and a float with too little travel between on and off makes the pump short cycle dozens of times an hour. We watch the float through a full cycle. Then we set the on and off points so the pump runs longer, less often.

    Home Sump Pump Service in Highlands Ranch

    A typical house pit is about 18 inches across and two feet deep, and most of what goes wrong happens inside it. We pull the pump, clear the intake screen and scoop out the silt. Then we run a full water test. Basements are common in Colorado, and shifting soil can crack floor drains, sewer lines, and foundation walls over time.

    Annual Sump Pump Check-Ups in Highlands Ranch

    Once a year is the minimum. We check the motor, impeller, float and discharge line on every annual visit. Then we fill the pit and time how long the pump takes to empty it, because a slow drawdown is the earliest sign of a worn impeller or a partly blocked line.

    Discharge Line and Check Valve Service in Highlands Ranch

    The check valve sits on the discharge pipe a foot or so above the pump and keeps the water in the line from falling back into the pit. When it fails, the pump pumps the same water twice. Motors wear out faster. We test the valve, look for a weep hole between the pump and the valve, and check every joint up to the wall.
