    Frequently Asked Questions About Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Highlands Ranch

    A minor, accessible pinhole repair starts around $200. Slab leaks run higher. Complex repairs like under-slab leaks can run past $2,000, with location, severity and footage of new pipe setting most of the price. You get a written number after locating.

    Wet spots on walls, ceilings or floors are the obvious sign. Pressure drops count too. The sound of running water when nothing is on, a bill that jumps, or blue-green crust on exposed fittings all point to copper that is leaking or about to. The meter confirms it.

    Wall thickness. K is thickest. Type K is used for underground service lines, Type L is the standard for interior water lines in many codes, while Type M is lighter and more prone to pinholes over time. We repair with Type L.

    Insurance often covers sudden water damage. Worn pipe is usually excluded. The cost of repairing old or corroded copper is usually yours, but the resulting damage to the house may be claimable. Check with your agent. We provide documentation.

    If you have had repeated leaks, it is worth pricing. PEX does not pinhole. PEX resists the corrosion and scale that cause copper pinholes and goes in quickly with fewer wall openings, so a PEX repipe often beats years of patching. We price both options.

    Keep water pressure under about 80 psi. A regulator helps. Treating hard or aggressive water, insulating pipe in cold spots and limiting how long a recirculation pump runs slow down the corrosion that thins copper. Know where your main shutoff is.
