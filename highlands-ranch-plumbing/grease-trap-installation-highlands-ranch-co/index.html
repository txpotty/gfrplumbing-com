    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Installation in Highlands Ranch

    Outdoor gravity interceptors run from 750 gallons to well past 2,000, and setting one means an excavator on site and a traffic-rated lid if it sits in the drive lane.     Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Highlands Ranch

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Highlands Ranch

    Expect roughly $1,500 to $4,000 for a small indoor trap and $7,000 to $20,000 or more for an outdoor gravity interceptor. A floor-recessed trap costs more than one set on the floor. Location drives the rest. A tank under a parking lot needs a traffic-rated lid and asphalt repair, which a tank in a grass strip does not.

    We start with a fixture list and the drain rate of each fixture. Each sink's volume and drain time give a flow rate, and the dish machine's spec sheet gives its flow. That gives the GPM figure. Indoor units match the GPM, and outdoor tanks get sized by volume and retention.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. The trade usually calls the small indoor unit a trap and the big outdoor tank an interceptor. Codes vary on the wording. Many codes say hydromechanical interceptor and gravity interceptor instead, and we match the local wording on the permit.

    You get the manufacturer's warranty on the unit and a written labor warranty from us on the installation. We register the unit with the maker so the factory warranty is on record. Keep your cleaning logs. Some manufacturers ask for proof of regular service before they honor a claim on a cracked body or a failed baffle.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans. We file both when needed and give you copies of the approvals, which your health inspector may ask to see at opening. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    Watch the pump-out bills. A trap that needs service far more often than it used to is either too small or broken inside. Smell is another clue. Slow drains near the trap and failed inspections point to replacement too.

    Size comes from flow, and flow comes from your fixtures. We add up the drain rate of each sink compartment, floor sink and dish machine, then apply the code's drain-down time to get gallons per minute. Gravity tanks use volume instead. Outdoor tanks get sized by the sewer authority's formula, which we run for you.

    Coverage comes from two places. The manufacturer warrants the trap body and lid, and the term depends on the brand and whether it is steel or plastic. Our written labor warranty covers the joints we made and how we set the unit. Damage from skipped cleanings falls outside it.
