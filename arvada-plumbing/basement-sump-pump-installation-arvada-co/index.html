    Commercial and Multi-Unit Sump Installations in Arvada

    Office buildings, parking structures and multi-unit properties with below-grade levels usually need duplex sump systems with two pumps and an alternating control panel.     Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Arvada

    A great pump is useless if the water it moves runs right back toward the foundation. We route rigid PVC discharge pipe out of the basement and extend it at least 10 feet from the house or into a pop-up emitter or dry well. A spring or swing check valve keeps the column of water from dropping back into the pit after every cycle, which protects the motor. Hard freezes are a normal part of winter in Colorado, and pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, and garages are especially vulnerable. Where lines can freeze, we add a freeze-resistant outlet so ice cannot block the line.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Arvada

    Pedestal sump pumps are an good choice for narrow or shallow sump pits, as their motor sits above the water level. This design makes them easier to maintain and can sometimes offer a longer lifespan because the motor stays out of the water. GFR Plumbing can properly install pedestal sump pumps in your Arvada home, with the right sizing and setup for effective water removal without crowding your pit.

    In many areas, including Arvada, a plumbing permit may be required for new sump pump installations or major replacements to confirm compliance with local building codes. GFR Plumbing will help you work through the permit process and make sure all work is up to code.

    A standard basement sump pump installation usually takes 4 to 8 hours. If a new sump pit needs to be excavated, or there are unforeseen challenges with plumbing or electrical access, the job might stretch to a full day. Our team works efficiently to limit disruption to your home.

    Homes from the late 1970s through the mid 1990s may still have polybutylene supply lines, which are known to fail at fittings. Homes built on slabs without basements rarely need one.     While it might seem like a DIY project, basement sump pump installation involves real plumbing, electrical work and proper discharge routing.     Watch for a pump that runs constantly, cycles on and off rapidly, makes grinding noises or never seems to turn on. Rust stains, a stuck float and water sitting above the switch level are also red flags.
