    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Lafayette

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. They can mean the wall is bowing from soil pressure, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Vertical cracks are usually a one-visit job.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Lafayette, CO

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Dry air, intense sun, and big daily temperature swings put steady stress on pipes, fittings, and outdoor plumbing in Colorado. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then seal the face with a masonry waterproofer that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. Most homeowners notice the musty smell is gone within a few weeks. The weep holes empty into interior drain tile when there is one.

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Lafayette, CO

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Housing in Colorado ranges from older homes with galvanized and cast iron pipe to fast-growing suburbs built with plastic and PEX. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. Sealing soft old brick with a rigid coating traps water inside it and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with interior drain tile. Old mortar joints get repointed first.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Lafayette

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most reliable fix for a leaking basement, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing to carry the water away from the wall. Rocky mountain ground makes trenching for sewer and water lines harder, and lines must be buried below the local frost depth. Backfill goes in last.
