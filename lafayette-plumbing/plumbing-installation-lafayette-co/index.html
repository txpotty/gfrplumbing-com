    Whole-House Water Softener Installation in Lafayette

    Many larger systems in Colorado draw on mountain snowmelt that runs fairly soft, while plains communities and groundwater sources often deliver harder water. Softener sizing comes down to hardness times daily gallons, which tells us how many grains the resin has to remove between regenerations. Undersize it and it regenerates all the time, burning through salt. We set the drain line with an air gap. Programming follows, set to your hardness, and we run one test cycle.

    Gas Line Installation for New Appliances in Lafayette, CO

    Water heaters, ranges, dryers, fireplaces and pool heaters all pull from the same gas service. Each branch is sized from the total BTU load and the run length so the last appliance on the line is not starved. New lines get pressure tested, always. Bigger loads sometimes need a meter upgrade, and we set that up with the gas utility.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Lafayette, CO

    A basement bathroom sits below the sewer line, so gravity cannot carry the waste out. An ejector pump in a sealed basin lifts it up to the main drain. Pump and basin are sized to the fixtures, the basin is vented to code, and the discharge gets a check valve and a shutoff so the pump can come out for service. We usually install Liberty or Zoeller pumps.

    Sump Pump and Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Lafayette

    Basements and crawl spaces that take on water need a sump pump, and fixtures below the sewer line need an ejector. One moves groundwater, the other moves sewage, and they are not interchangeable. We size before we sell. We match pump capacity to how fast the pit fills, put the pump on its own circuit and run the discharge with a check valve.

    Tankless Water Heater Installation in Lafayette, CO

    Navien and Rinnai tankless units hang on a wall and free up the floor space a tank used to take. Gas models usually need a bigger gas line and new stainless or PVC venting. Electric models need amperage. Sizing is based on flow in gallons per minute and how far the incoming water has to be heated. Higher elevations lower the boiling point of water, which affects water heater settings and relief valve performance in mountain homes. Every unit gets isolation valves so it can be flushed.

    Backflow Assembly Testing and Installation in Lafayette

    Fire lines and irrigation systems account for most of the backflow assemblies we install. Each one is tested right after installation by a certified backflow tester, and the report goes to the water purveyor. That starts the annual clock. Retesting is usually yearly, and we keep a reminder list so you do not miss the date.

    Backflow Preventer Installation in Lafayette

    Irrigation systems and fire sprinkler lines are the usual reasons a home needs a backflow preventer. It keeps fertilizer-laced lawn water from being pulled back into the drinking supply when a main break or hydrant flush drops pressure. The type matters. We match the assembly to the hazard and the local rule, then test it before we leave.

    PEX Repiping in Lafayette

    Copper repipes cost more and take longer, which is why most whole-house repipes today use PEX-A or PEX-B tubing with brass or poly fittings. You also hear less of it, since PEX softens water hammer. It also handles a freeze better. Before leaving a home for a winter trip, keep the heat at a safe setting and consider shutting off the main water supply. Fittings are matched to the tubing manufacturer.
