    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Water Heater Repair in Lafayette

    Expect a range of $150 to $800 in Lafayette, CO. The part and the access decide where in that range you fall. Simple jobs like a thermocouple sit near the bottom, while a gas control valve, a power vent blower or a flue rebuild pushes the bill toward the top end of the range. The quote comes before the work.

    A thermocouple is a small copper-sheathed rod that sits in the pilot flame and makes a tiny electric current. That current holds a magnet in the gas valve open. If the flame dies, the current stops. When the pilot goes out, the magnet lets go and the valve shuts off gas to both the pilot and the burner, which is the safety that keeps raw gas from filling a closet. Weak thermocouples cause more pilot outages than anything else.

    Swapping a thermocouple, a pilot assembly or a gas valve usually does not need one. A permit typically comes into play when the heater is replaced or the gas line or vent is altered. The Lafayette building department sets the exact rules, and when a permit applies we pull it, schedule the inspection and meet the inspector so you do not have to take a morning off. Inspections are usually quick.

    Lukewarm water, a pilot that keeps going out and a rumbling noise from the bottom of the tank are the usual early signs of trouble. Water around the base or rust in the hot water means the tank itself may be failing. A sulfur smell is a separate matter. Treat it as a gas leak until someone tests it.

    A weak thermocouple is the usual cause. A plugged pilot orifice can also make the flame too small to heat it. On vapor-resistant tanks, a lint-clogged flame arrestor can starve the burner and trip the thermal switch, which looks like a pilot outage from the outside. Garage drafts can snuff out an older pilot.

We check draft on every repair with a smoke test or a combustion analyzer, and we look for heat-scorched paint or melted plastic near the draft hood, which are signs of spillage. A carbon monoxide alarm belongs on each floor of the house.

    For part swaps, almost never. Thermocouples, igniters and gas valves are treated as repairs by most building departments in Colorado. Replacing the heater or altering the gas piping or vent typically does need one. We sort that out before the work starts.
