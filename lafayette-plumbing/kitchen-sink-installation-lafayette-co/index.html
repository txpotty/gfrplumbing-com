    How We Install Kitchen Sinks in Lafayette, CO: Style by Style

    Double Bowl Sink and Continuous Waste in Lafayette

    A double bowl drains both sides through one trap, using either a center outlet continuous waste or an end outlet tee. We pick the layout around your disposal and dishwasher, since a disposal on one side changes where the tee can go and how much fall each side gets. Heavy-wall fittings resist leaks.

    New Construction Sink Rough-In in Lafayette, CO

    Rough-in sets the drain height and the stops long before the cabinets arrive. We usually set the kitchen drain stub around 16 to 20 inches off the floor, then adjust for deep bowls or a disposal so the trap does not end up below the wall outlet. Venting happens at this stage. Having the sink and faucet specs early saves moving pipe later.

    Commercial Kitchen Sink Installation in Lafayette, CO

    Three-compartment sinks, prep sinks and hand sinks each have their own health code rules. Prep sinks usually need an indirect drain to a floor sink with an air gap, while the three-compartment sink often runs through the grease trap. We confirm the rules first. Work is scheduled before or after service so the kitchen does not close.

    Drop-In Kitchen Sink Installation in Lafayette

    Sitting on the counter by their rim, drop-in sinks are a natural fit for laminate, butcher block and tile. It is often the quickest swap. After checking the cutout, we seal the rim and draw the clips down evenly, and in older homes and rentals it is often a two-hour job.

    Disposal and Dishwasher Hookup with Sink Installation in Lafayette, CO

    Most new kitchen sinks get paired with a disposal and a dishwasher. We mount the disposal, run the dishwasher drain through an air gap or high loop and install a dedicated hot supply with its own shutoff. One visit costs less than three. We test it all the way you will use it.

    Undermount Sink Installation in Lafayette, CO

    Undermounts hang from clips anchored into the underside of the stone, plus a bead of silicone along the rim. We support the bowl from below with a brace while the silicone cures, usually overnight, before loading it with water. Skipping the cure time leads to sagging. A sink that drops even an eighth of an inch opens a gap where water and crumbs collect along the counter edge.

    Granite and Quartz Composite Sinks in Lafayette

    Composite sinks are heavier than stainless and usually thicker at the drain, so standard strainers sometimes need a longer threaded body. We check the strainer fit first. When the strainer is too short for the sink thickness, we replace it instead of cranking it down and chipping the drain opening.

    Undermount Kitchen Sink Installation in Lafayette

    Undermount sinks rely on clips and adhesive to stay attached to granite or quartz. The bowl is mounted, the rim sealed with silicone and the sink braced while the adhesive sets, normally overnight before it can take a full bowl of water. Then come the strainers and trap.
