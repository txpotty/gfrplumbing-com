    Pulling a Submersible Pump in Lafayette

    Pulling a submersible means lifting the pump and every length of drop pipe out through the pitless adapter, sometimes hundreds of feet of it. We check the torque arrestor and every splice on the way up. Most jobs are done in a day.

    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Lafayette

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes a healthy pump look sick. Short cycling, where the pump starts every few seconds of water use, usually means the tank bladder has failed and the pump is paying for it. A tire gauge on the air valve tells us a lot. The air charge should read 2 psi under the switch cut-in.

    Drop Pipe and Wire Replacement in Lafayette

    Galvanized drop pipe from the 1970s and 1980s rusts from the inside, and a pinhole at 150 feet will bleed pressure every time the pump shuts off. We replace it with schedule 120 PVC or poly pipe, depending on the depth and the pump weight. Wire gets the same look. Cracked insulation and taped splices get cut out.

    Jet Pump Seal and Motor Replacement in Lafayette, CO

    Single-stage and two-stage jet pumps wear out at the seal and impeller, and a leaking shaft seal shows up as a puddle under the motor. New pump, primed and tested. After that, we watch the pressure gauge cycle from cut-in to cut-out.

    Emergency Well Pump Service in Lafayette, CO

    Lost water at the house? Call (720) 727-9398 and tell us what the pressure gauge reads. The gauge reading, with the pump running or silent, tells us whether to bring a pump or just a control box. We try to restore water the same day.

    Constant Pressure Pump Systems in Lafayette

    Franklin SubDrive and Grundfos SQE are the two constant pressure systems we install most. Both use a small 2 to 20 gallon tank instead of a big one, since the drive keeps the pump running at a matched speed. Soft starts also cut wear on the motor. The upfront cost is higher.

    No-Water Calls in Lafayette

    Pump trouble rarely gives notice. One morning the faucet coughs air and then nothing, and by then the motor has often been struggling for weeks. Before we pull the pump, we test the switch and control box.

    Sandy Wells and Submersible Pumps in Lafayette

    Sand kills submersibles early. Grit in a well chews up impellers fast, so we look for it on the old pump and may set the new one higher or add a sand separator. Pump setting depth matters.
