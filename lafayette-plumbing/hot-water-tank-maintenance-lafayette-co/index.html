    What a Tank Tune-Up Covers in Lafayette, CO

    Anode Rod Replacement in Lafayette, CO

    Water chemistry changes from one street to the next, and so does anode life. Most rods last three to five years. A softened water supply can chew through a magnesium rod in two. A new rod costs a fraction of a tank and often adds years.

    Electric Element Check in Lafayette

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. Each element gets an ohm reading. If the hot water quits halfway through a shower, the lower element is the first suspect. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    Burner and Pilot Service in Lafayette, CO

    On a gas tank, a dirty burner throws a lazy yellow flame and leaves soot on the flue baffle. The burner comes out and gets brushed and vacuumed. A tired thermocouple drops the pilot, so we test it too. The goal is a steady blue flame.

    Maintenance Versus Replacement Costs in Lafayette, CO

    A yearly flush costs a small fraction of a new installed tank, which often runs $1,400 to $3,000 or more depending on size and venting. We tell you which one makes sense. Past 10 to 12 years, rust at the base or a seam leak means replacement. Younger tanks with a sound liner are worth maintaining.

    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Lafayette, CO

    The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. Many larger systems in Colorado draw on mountain snowmelt that runs fairly soft, while plains communities and groundwater sources often deliver more mineral-heavy water. We break the hex head loose, pull the rod and check how much bare wire shows. If six inches of wire is bare, it is time for a new one.

    Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Lafayette

    Street pressure over 80 psi is hard on every fixture in the house, and plumbing codes generally call for a pressure reducing valve above that number. We check static pressure at a hose bib. The relief valve lever gets lifted next. If the valve opens and reseats cleanly and the expansion tank holds its charge, the tank is safe from pressure spikes.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Lafayette

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. The new valve must match the tank's BTU or kilowatt rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. Never plug or cap that pipe.

    Relief Valve Testing in Lafayette

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. Our tech lifts the lever, lets water flow, then watches it close. If it keeps dripping, we replace it. We also confirm the discharge pipe runs full size to within six inches of the floor or to an approved drain, which is what plumbing codes ask for.
