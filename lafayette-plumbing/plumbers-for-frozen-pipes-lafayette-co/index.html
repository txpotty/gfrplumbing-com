    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Lafayette

    Costs range from about $200 for simple thawing to $1,500 or more for a burst pipe inside a finished wall. Access drives the price.     Pipe location matters more than brand. Metal pipe cracks more easily. Older galvanized steel and copper in uninsulated attics, crawlspaces and outside walls are the most likely to split, while PEX in the same spot may freeze but often survives, though its fittings can still fail.

    You can open the faucet and gently warm an exposed pipe with a hair dryer. Open flame is never safe here. Hidden pipes are better left to us, since a hidden split can flood the wall as it thaws.

    Disconnect hoses, cover outdoor faucets and backflow preventers, and let a faucet drip on very cold nights. Leave sink cabinet doors open. If you leave town during cold weather, keep the heat at 55 degrees or higher and consider shutting off the main and draining the lines, which is the most reliable way to avoid coming home to a flooded house.

    A faucet that trickles or runs dry after a cold night is the most common sign. Visible frost on a pipe confirms it. A toilet that will not refill or a bulging pipe also points to ice.

    A new freeze is not a warranty issue, but it is a warning sign. The line is still too cold. We will look at why it froze again and quote insulation, heat cable or a reroute into a heated space, because patching the same exposed run every winter costs more than fixing the cause once.

    Thawing and small pipe repairs usually do not need a permit. Larger jobs can. Repiping, rerouting or replacing a backflow device may need one, which we handle.
