    Water Line Upgrades and Code Compliance in Denver, CO

    A lot of older houses were fed with a 1/2-inch or 3/4-inch line that was fine for one bathroom. It cannot keep up with two baths and a washer running.     PEX Water Line Installation in Denver, CO

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Hard freezes are a normal part of winter in Colorado, and pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, and garages are especially vulnerable. A frozen PEX line often survives where copper would crack. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and keep it covered from sunlight at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.

    PEX Water Line Replacement in Denver

    PEX costs less than copper and goes in faster, which matters on a long run. Scale and corrosion are not a problem with it. We use PEX-a or PEX rated for direct burial, use only fittings listed for burial, and lay tracer wire on top so a locator can find it later, since plastic pipe does not show up on a metal detector. Cheap insurance.

    Main Water Line Repair vs. Replacement in Denver

    One leak on a twenty-year-old copper line? A repair coupling is often the right call. Three leaks on a galvanized line from the 1950s is different, because the pipe is thin everywhere and the next leak is already forming, so the full line costs less than a string of repairs. We tell you which camp you are in and show you the pipe we cut out.

    Traditional Water Line Excavation in Denver

    Sometimes the open trench wins. A collapsed line, a route with sharp bends, or solid rock can rule out trenchless.     Copper Water Line Installation in Denver

    Type K copper is the thick-wall grade made for burial. It handles high street pressure without complaint. We bed it in sand, keep it away from sharp rock, and sleeve it where it passes through a foundation wall, because concrete and copper rubbing together for twenty years will wear through the pipe. It costs more than PEX.
