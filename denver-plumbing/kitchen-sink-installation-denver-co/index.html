    How We Install Kitchen Sinks in Denver, CO: The Details

    Workstation Sink Installation in Denver, CO

    Workstation sinks have a ledge for cutting boards, colanders and drying racks, and most are single deep bowls with an offset drain. We plan the trap around that offset drain so the disposal fits and the cabinet keeps usable space on one side. Check the faucet reach as well.

    Restaurant Sink Installation in Denver

    Restaurants, cafes and commissary kitchens in Denver get their commercial sinks installed by GFR Plumbing. Hand sinks need tempered or hot and cold water. Three-compartment, prep and hand sinks go in with the floor sinks, indirect drains and grease trap tie-ins the inspector checks.

    Farmhouse Sink Plumbing in Denver, CO

    Fireclay and cast iron farmhouse sinks can weigh well over 100 pounds empty, before a single drop of water goes in. A support frame goes into the cabinet and the sink is leveled before the countertop goes on. A deep bowl means a lower trap. We plan the drain for that depth from the start.

    Drop-In Kitchen Sink Installation in Denver

    Sitting on the counter by their rim, drop-in sinks are a natural fit for laminate, butcher block and tile. Usually the fastest upgrade. After checking the cutout, we seal the rim and draw the clips down evenly, and in older homes and rentals it is often a two-hour job.

    Undermount Kitchen Sink Installation in Denver

    An undermount depends on its clips and adhesive to stay attached to granite or quartz. We mount the bowl, seal the rim with silicone and brace it while the adhesive sets, which usually takes overnight before the sink can hold a full load of water. Strainers and traps go in after.

    Kitchen Sink Installation Cost Factors in Denver, CO

    Undermount and farmhouse installs cost more than a drop-in swap. Each extra, like a new disposal, a moved drain, a lowered wall stub or replacing galvanized stubs, adds labor and shows up as its own line on the quote. Plumbing is priced apart from the counter.

    Top-mount Sink Installation in Denver

    Drop-in sinks are common in Colorado homes with laminate or tile counters. The rim holds the sink up. After checking the cutout, we lay silicone under the lip and draw the clips down evenly so the whole rim sits flat. Next we connect and test the faucet and drain.

    New Construction Sink Rough-In in Denver, CO

    The rough-in decides where the drain and valves end up long before the cabinets arrive. We usually set the kitchen drain stub around 16 to 20 inches off the floor, then adjust for deep bowls or a disposal so the trap does not end up below the wall outlet. Vents go in now. Having the sink and faucet specs early saves moving pipe later.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Kitchen Sink Installation in Denver

    Most drop-in installs run $300 to $600 for labor. Undermount and farmhouse installs typically run $450 to $1,200, and adding a disposal or moving the drain adds another $150 to $600 depending on the work. The price is fixed up front.

    Rim leaks that recaulking will not stop, cracks, chipped enamel, rust spots and stains that never clean up all point to replacement. Chips in cast iron start to rust. After a chip reaches bare iron, rust works under the enamel and the damage keeps spreading.

    Begin with your cabinet width and the current cutout. Then think about bowl depth, single or double bowl, the material and whether you want a disposal, since all of those change the plumbing under the sink. Some finishes show hard water more.

    An experienced DIYer can handle a plain drop-in swap. Undermount, farmhouse and double-bowl installs with a disposal are where most DIY jobs leak, usually at the strainer, a slip joint or the disposal flange. A slow drip will rot the base.

    Two to four hours for most standard swaps. Farmhouse sinks, cabinet reinforcement or replacing old drain lines can push the job to a full day, and undermounts also need overnight cure time before the drain is loaded.

    Most stock sink cabinets need reinforcement. We add a support frame under fireclay and cast iron sinks so the weight rests on the cabinet sides, not the face frame or the countertop.

    Look for persistent leaks, heavy rust, cracks or deep chips. Clogs that keep returning usually mean a drain line problem, though a corroded or loose strainer can also be behind them. We look first, then recommend.
