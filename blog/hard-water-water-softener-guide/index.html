    Hard Water in Colorado: When a Water Softener Pays for Itself

    By Maria Delgado · September 27, 2026 · 5 min read

    What Hard Water Means in Colorado

    Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium picked up from rock and soil, and labs measure it in grains per gallon. Water above roughly 7 grains per gallon counts as hard. Once it passes 10 grains, crust shows up on faucets and spots on glassware within a few weeks. A cheap test strip gives a rough number.

    Mountain homes in Colorado frequently rely on private wells, which may need treatment for iron, manganese, or natural minerals.

    Your water provider posts a yearly quality report that lists hardness, often in milligrams per liter. Divide that number by 17.1 to get grains per gallon. Wells vary more. A well owner should pay for a full lab test, because iron and manganese in the water change which softener will actually work.

    What Hard Water Costs You

    The water heater takes the worst of it. In a gas tank, scale piles up on the bottom and blocks heat from the burner. You hear it as popping or rumbling when the burner runs, and the tank burns more gas to heat the same water. Electric elements crust over and burn out early.

    Scale also works on everything else that touches water. Single-handle faucet cartridges get stiff and begin dripping. Inlet screens on the dishwasher and washer plug up, and in a few years a showerhead can lose half its spray pattern to scale. You also use more soap.

    • Water heater: shorter life and higher fuel use
    • Faucets and valves: worn cartridges and drips
    • Tankless units: scale in the heat exchanger and error codes
    • Laundry: stiff towels and more detergent

    When a Softener Pays for Itself

    Run the numbers on your own house. A tank water heater that lasts 8 years on hard water can reach 12 or more on softened water. Add the soap savings and fewer faucet repairs. With four people and water harder than 10 grains, the typical system we put in pays back in about five to seven years.

    Tankless water heaters change the math. On hard water, most makers call for a yearly descale, and some will deny a warranty claim for scale damage. A softener can stretch that to every few years. Check your warranty paperwork for a hardness limit, often around 11 or 12 grains.

    Picking the Right Softener

    Size the softener by grains removed per day, not by the box. Multiply your hardness by the gallons your household uses each day, which is about 75 gallons per person. Four people at 12 grains works out to around 3,600 grains a day, which a 32,000 to 40,000 grain unit handles. Oversized units waste salt.

    • Metered regeneration: regenerates based on gallons used and uses less salt
    • Timer regeneration: cheaper, but regenerates on a schedule even when it is not needed
    • Salt-free conditioners: reduce scale buildup but do not remove hardness

    Salt-free conditioners get a lot of advertising. They change the way the minerals behave so less scale sticks to pipes and heaters, but the calcium is still sitting in the water. You will still see spots on glass, and a test strip will read the same as before. Above about 15 grains, only an ion-exchange softener takes the hardness out.

    Getting a Softener Installed

    A softener needs a drain for the brine cycle and a bypass valve, so you can take it offline without shutting off the house. We usually put it on the main line in the garage, and it takes about half a day. We often leave the hose bibs on unsoftened water so you are not spending salt on the lawn. Ask for a hardness test first.
