    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Parker

    Standard drain snaking in Parker, CO usually costs $150 to $500 depending on where the clog sits and how easy it is to reach. Mains with heavy roots, or jobs that need jetting, run $400 to $1,000 and sometimes more. Camera work is extra. We quote after we see the setup, so you know the cost before the cable goes in.

    Every rooter job from GFR Plumbing carries a guarantee for a set period on the line we cleared. We go over the terms before we start. It covers the line we worked on. Should that line clog again during the guarantee window, the return visit is free.

    A limited guarantee for a set number of days comes with most rooter work. A repeat clog in that window means a free return visit.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. A jetter uses water at thousands of psi to scour the inside of the pipe from wall to wall. Jetting leaves the pipe cleaner. Snaking handles one-time clogs, while jetting is for grease lines and scaled mains that keep coming back.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. They can soften old PVC glue joints and pit cast iron over time. They are dangerous to handle. A cable takes the clog out instead of melting a narrow path through it, which keeps the drain open longer.

    Routine drain cleaning in Parker, CO does not usually need a permit. Major repair, sewer replacement or excavation in the yard generally does. We take care of it. If the rooter work turns up a problem that needs a permit, we tell you before any repair gets scheduled.

    Slow drains, gurgling pipes and sewer smell are the early ones. If the tub fills when the toilet flushes, the main is blocked. That one needs a call. More than one fixture draining slow at the same time usually means the main, not a branch.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe walls clean. Many tough lines do best with both, cut first to open the line and jetted second to wash out what the blades left.
