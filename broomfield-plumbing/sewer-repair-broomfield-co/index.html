    After-Hours Sewer Repair in Broomfield

    Main lines like to fail on holidays. We still answer.     Sewer Repair Cost Comparison in Broomfield, CO

    A spot repair is the cheapest fix when the rest of the line is sound. Per foot, lining runs higher. It avoids excavation, though, and on long runs under driveways or patios the savings on concrete and landscaping often outweigh the higher price of the liner itself. Full replacement is right for Orangeburg or a line broken in several places. You get each option in writing with a number attached.

    Sewer Pipe Relining in Broomfield, CO

    Relining builds a new pipe inside the old one. We clean the line with a jetter first. A flexible liner saturated with epoxy is pulled or inverted into place, inflated against the old wall and left to cure, so every crack and leaking joint is covered by one jointless pipe. The finished pipe loses a little diameter. For most houses the flow difference is negligible.

    Sewer Pipe Replacement Options in Broomfield, CO

    Replacement makes sense when the camera shows failures every few feet. Patching that line is wasted money. We price open trench replacement, pipe bursting and full-length lining side by side for your Colorado property, showing what each method costs and what it does to the yard. Depth, access and what sits on top of the line decide it.

    Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in Broomfield

    Some lines are too far gone for a liner. Crushed pipe cannot be lined. When the camera shows a collapse, a large offset or a belly deep enough to hold several inches of water, we open a trench, remove the old pipe and lay new Schedule 40 PVC on proper bedding at a quarter inch of fall per foot. We see this most in older neighborhoods with clay pipe. We stage the work so you lose sewer service for a few hours, not days.

    Orangeburg and Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Broomfield

    Orangeburg is wood fiber soaked in coal tar. It flattens with age. Cast iron from the same era rusts from the inside, builds scale and eventually splits along the bottom where the sewage runs, and once the camera shows failures along the whole run, patching it stops being worth the money. We replace these lines with PVC. Trenchless bursting is often an option.

    Emergency Sewer Repair in Broomfield

    Raw sewage on a basement floor is not a next-week problem. We answer at night and on weekends. The first goal on an emergency call is to get the line flowing, usually with a cable or jetter, and then camera it to find out whether you have a clog or a structural failure that will back up again tomorrow. Sometimes the permanent fix waits for daylight. In the meantime, we flag which drains to avoid.

    Tree Root Invasion Solutions in Broomfield

    Roots in a sewer line are not the tree's fault. A leaking joint invited them in. Warm, wet, nutrient-rich air escaping from a loose joint draws roots toward the line, and once they get in they keep growing until the joint splits or the pipe cracks open. We cut them out, then talk about the long-term fix. A liner or new pipe at that joint is what actually stops regrowth.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Repair in Broomfield

    Most jobs fall between $500 for a small spot repair and about $3,000 for a trenchless section. Full replacement costs more. A complete open trench replacement can reach $10,000 or more, depending on the length of the line, how deep it sits and what has to be removed and restored on top of it. The written price comes after we see the pipe.

    Multiple drains backing up at once is the big one. The tub often shows it first. Water rising in the shower when the washer drains, a sewage smell near the cleanout, and wet or very green patches over the line tell you the trouble is in the main line, not one fixture. Video makes it certain.

    Most spot repairs are done in one day, excavation and backfill included. Some take longer. Larger replacements, or jobs that need a right-of-way permit to dig near the street, usually run two to four days with inspection timing as the main variable. We put the schedule in writing.

    One slow sink is usually a local clog. Multiple fixtures point to the main. If the toilet bubbles when the tub drains, or the lowest drain in the house backs up whenever someone runs water upstairs, you are looking at a problem in the main line downstream of every branch. Time to put a camera in it.

    Schedule 40 PVC is our standard for open trench work. It does not rust. For pipe bursting we use fused HDPE, which comes in continuous lengths with no joints for roots to find, and for lining we use epoxy liners rated for decades of service inside the old pipe. We follow what your city's code allows.

    Flush only toilet paper. Flushable wipes are not. Keep grease out of the kitchen sink, and if your house has mature trees near the line or older clay or cast iron pipe, have it checked on video every couple of years to catch problems early. Annual jetting helps on lines with known root trouble.

    It depends on the city and the scope. Replacements usually need one. Small repairs on private property may be exempt, but work in the public right-of-way or at the main connection nearly always requires a permit and an inspection. We handle the permit when one applies.
