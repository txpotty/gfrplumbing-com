    Waterproofing for Older Colorado Homes in Broomfield, CO

    Older basements were often built with block walls and no exterior membrane.     Foundation Crack Repair in Broomfield

    Hairline cracks in a poured wall are normal and usually dry, but a crack that seeps after rain needs attention. Rocky mountain ground makes trenching for sewer and water lines slower, and lines must be buried below the local frost depth. Polyurethane injection from the inside fills a leaking crack all the way through and flexes with the wall. Most injections take an hour or two.

    Drain and Sewer Pipe Leak Repair in Broomfield, CO

    Cast iron drain stacks in older homes crack and scale from the inside out. We cut out cracked cast iron and splice in new pipe with shielded no-hub couplings. Plastic joints can fail too. A separated PVC joint usually means the pipe was not fully seated or not supported.

    Wall Crack Sealing for Basements in Broomfield

    Injection works on poured concrete walls, not block. Ports go along the crack, the face gets sealed, and resin goes in from the bottom up until it fills the full depth. Epoxy bonds the two sides. Polyurethane is better for active leaks because it foams on contact with water.

    Exterior Membrane Waterproofing in Broomfield, CO

    Excavating the outside of a foundation lets us fix the leak where the water starts. Higher elevations lower the boiling point of water, which affects water heater settings and relief valve performance in mountain homes. The membrane is matched to those conditions, the footing drain is replaced, and the backfill is graded to slope away. Downspouts get extended too.

    Sump Pit and Pump Installation in Broomfield

    Installing a new sump means breaking out a section of slab, setting a lined pit, and running the discharge out through the rim joist. We use a cast iron pump in most homes and a 1-1/2 inch discharge with a check valve. Discharge cannot go into the sewer in most places. It has to discharge outside, far enough from the foundation that it does not cycle back into the pit, often ten feet or more.
