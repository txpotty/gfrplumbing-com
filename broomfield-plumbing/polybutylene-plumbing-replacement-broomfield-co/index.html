    Understanding Polybutylene Failure in Broomfield

    Polybutylene's inherent weakness stems from its chemical reaction with oxidants like chlorine, commonly used in municipal water treatment. Over time, this reaction causes the pipes and their plastic fittings to become brittle, leading to hairline cracks and eventual rupture. Mountain homes in Colorado frequently rely on private wells, which may need treatment for iron, manganese, or other minerals. Hard water can also add stress to pipe that is already breaking down. Understanding this failure mechanism is crucial for diagnosing issues and recommending the most effective and permanent replacement strategy for your home.

    Full Home Repiping with PEX in Broomfield

    When a home with polybutylene pipes sits on a slab foundation, repiping becomes a more complex process. Our team specializes in full home repiping using PEX (cross-linked polyethylene), a flexible, durable, and corrosion-resistant material. PEX is an excellent choice for Colorado homes, as it resists freezing better than rigid pipes and expands to accommodate temperature changes. We carefully plan the repipe to minimize disruption, often running new lines through attics or existing wall cavities, delivering a complete and long-lasting upgrade for your entire plumbing system.

    Whole-Home PEX Replacement in Broomfield, CO

    PEX-A is the most common replacement for polybutylene because it is flexible, scale resistant and easy to route. We use Uponor PEX-A with expansion fittings that do not restrict flow. New lines run from the main shutoff to every fixture, water heater and appliance. Most homes are completed in two to four days.
