    Frequently Asked Questions About Drainage Services in Aurora

    Most basic drain clearing in Aurora, CO runs $150 to $500. A main line costs more than a sink. Hydro jetting runs higher than cabling because of the equipment and time, and sewer line repair can range from about $1,500 for a spot repair to well over $8,000 for a full replacement with digging.

    Snaking is the right first step for most single clogs. It costs less and takes less time. Jetting is better for grease-coated kitchen lines, scaled cast iron and roots that keep coming back, because it cleans the pipe wall instead of punching a hole through the blockage.

    Simple downspout extensions are a reasonable DIY project. Trench drains take more skill. A French drain or dry well needs correct slope, the right fabric and stone, and an outlet that will not dump water on a neighbor, and digging without an 811 locate risks hitting a gas or power line.

    Yes, sewer line repairs and replacements in Aurora, CO usually require a plumbing permit and an inspection. Clearing a clog needs no permit. We pull the permit, call 811 for locates, and schedule the inspection before backfilling so the inspector can see the new pipe, the bedding and the connections before they are buried.

    Drain cleaning usually means a cable machine with a cutting head that bores through the clog. It works fast and costs less. Jetting scours the full pipe wall, lasting longer, but it costs more and needs sound pipe.

    Installations like French drains, dry wells and sewer repairs carry a workmanship warranty. Drain clearing has a separate guarantee. If a drain we installed does not perform as promised, we fix it at no labor cost.

    A typical French drain or downspout system takes one to three days. Bigger jobs take longer. Projects with dry wells, long trench runs, or concrete that has to be saw-cut for a channel drain can take most of a week, especially if the ground is too wet to dig cleanly the first day.

    Clearing a clog does not need a permit. Repairs to the pipe itself often do. Replacing a section of sewer, adding a cleanout, or connecting a new drain to the sewer generally requires a permit and an inspection, and we handle that paperwork and schedule the inspector as part of the job.
