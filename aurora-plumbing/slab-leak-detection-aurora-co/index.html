    Cold Water Slab Leak Detection in Aurora, CO

    Cold-side leaks leave no warm spot. They are harder to see. They usually show up as a running meter, damp carpet, a musty smell or water seeping at baseboards, which is why we isolate the cold line, trace its route and listen along it with acoustic gear.

    Slab Leak Reroutes in Aurora

    Rerouting leaves the failed pipe in the slab. New pipe runs through the walls or attic instead, which avoids breaking concrete and often costs less than opening a finished floor. We usually use PEX because it handles heat and resists corrosion. Tile and hardwood floors make this the favorite.

    Acoustic Slab Leak Detection in Aurora

    Water escaping a pressurized pipe makes a hiss that carries up through concrete. A ground microphone picks it up. We move the pickup across the floor in a grid over the traced pipe route, finding where the sound is loudest and confirming it from another position, which works best on copper and less well on PEX.

    Post-Tension Slab Leak Detection in Aurora

    Many newer homes sit on post-tension slabs. These slabs have steel tendons stretched under tens of thousands of pounds of force, and cutting one by mistake is dangerous and can damage the foundation. We scan for tendons with ground-penetrating radar or a rebar locator before opening anything, or suggest a reroute that skips the slab.

    Why Locating First Saves Money in Aurora

    Detection usually costs a few hundred dollars. Guessing wrong costs far more. Breaking up a floor in the wrong place means paying for concrete work, flooring and a second opening, while an accurate mark keeps the repair to one small hole or a clean reroute that never touches the slab in your Aurora property.

    Listening Through Concrete in Aurora

    Ground microphones are sensitive. That means appliances, HVAC fans and traffic all show up in the headphones, so our techs shut off what they can and learn to tune out the rest, which is a skill that takes years to get good at. A pressurized line gives the clearest sound in your Aurora home.

    Slab Leaks in Older Homes in Aurora, CO

    Older homes in Aurora, CO often have soft copper or galvanized steel under the slab, and both corrode with age. Copper fails at bends and where it rubs concrete, while galvanized rusts from the inside until it splits. We know those failure points. That knowledge points the search at the likeliest spots first.
