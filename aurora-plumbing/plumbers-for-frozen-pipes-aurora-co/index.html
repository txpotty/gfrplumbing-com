    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Aurora

    Thawing an accessible frozen pipe in Aurora usually costs $150 to $400, and burst pipe repair typically runs $300 to $1,000. Freeze-proofing is cheaper. Insulating a crawlspace, adding heat cable to the worst run, and swapping outdoor faucets for frost-free models usually costs a few hundred dollars, which is less than a single burst pipe repair.

    Keep cold air away from the pipe. Seal and insulate around exposed lines. Close crawlspace vents for the season, insulate attic and garage runs, swap old hose bibs for frost-free models, and add self-regulating heat cable on any run that still gets too cold despite insulation.

    Insulate exposed pipes in attics, crawlspaces and garages, and seal air leaks around them. Pull hoses off outdoor faucets in fall. On the coldest nights, let a faucet on an outside wall drip, open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, and keep the thermostat at the same setting overnight instead of turning it down.

    Thawing and small pipe repairs usually do not need a permit. Larger jobs can. Repiping a section, rerouting a line into a heated wall, or replacing a backflow preventer may require a permit in many jurisdictions, and we pull it when needed so the work is on record.

    For an exposed pipe you can reach, yes, carefully. Open the faucet first. Warm the pipe with a hair dryer or a space heater kept a safe distance away, working from the faucet back toward the frozen section, and never use a torch, a propane heater or any open flame near framing.

    A short, exposed section usually thaws in 30 to 60 minutes. Pipes inside walls take more time. A frozen line inside an exterior wall or under a floor can take a few hours, especially if it is PEX or CPVC, which we thaw with gentler infrared or warm air instead of an electric thawing machine.

    You can open the faucet and gently warm an exposed pipe with a hair dryer. Never use a torch. If the frozen section is inside a wall, a ceiling or under the floor, call a plumber instead, because you cannot see whether it has already split, and thawing it can release water into the structure.

    No pipe is freeze-proof. PEX handles a freeze best. It can stretch slightly as ice forms instead of splitting the way copper does, but the fittings at each end can still crack, so location and insulation matter more than the pipe itself.
