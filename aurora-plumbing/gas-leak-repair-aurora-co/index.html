    What Our Gas Leak Repairs Cover in Aurora, CO

    Gas Line Replacement and Upgrades in Aurora, CO

    Adding a gas range or a tankless heater can overload pipe that was sized years ago for just a furnace and a water heater. We add up the BTU load and measure the run length before choosing a pipe size. Undersized pipe starves burners. Old pipe with heavy rust or bad threads gets replaced instead of patched, usually with black iron or CSST depending on access. A big tankless unit may also need a bigger meter from the utility.

    Gas Line Repair and Replacement in Aurora, CO

    Black iron pipe with threaded fittings is still the most common interior gas pipe, and the leak is nearly always at a thread, not in the middle of a length. The joint gets broken, cleaned and remade with new pipe dope rated for gas. Cracked fittings get replaced. Where a length has rusted badly, usually near a damp crawlspace vent, we cut back to sound pipe and splice in new. Every remade joint gets a detector check and a gauge test.

    Propane Gas Leak Repair in Aurora, CO

    Propane behaves differently from natural gas because it is heavier than air, so a leak settles into basements and crawlspaces instead of rising. The first-stage regulator at the tank and the second-stage regulator at the house both get checked. Low spots get sniffed first. If the supplier owns the tank, they handle the tank side and we handle the piping from there to the appliances. Rural properties with underground tanks get a line test too.

    Appliance Gas Line Repair in Aurora, CO

    Most appliance leaks show up at the flex connector or the shutoff valve behind the appliance, usually right at a flare nut where the two meet. Connectors get bent every time a range is pulled out to clean behind it. Valves weep at the stem. We swap in a new coated stainless connector, add a quarter-turn ball valve and set a sediment trap where the code calls for it. Each joint gets a detector pass and a bubble test before the pilot is lit again.

    Corrugated Stainless Steel Tubing Repair in Aurora, CO

    CSST is the yellow-jacketed flexible tubing used in many newer homes, and it can be damaged by a nearby lightning strike if it was never bonded. The bond is a heavy copper wire tying the gas pipe to the house ground. Plenty of older installs lack it. We repair CSST only with the matching brand's fittings, because TracPipe and Gastite parts do not interchange. A splice that skips that rule fails the inspection.

    Gas Leak Repair Cost Comparison in Aurora, CO

    Price follows access. A connector or fitting repair at an exposed appliance usually runs $150 to $450, testing included. Interior pipe inside finished walls or attics often lands between $300 and $1,000, and a buried yard line can run $1,000 to $4,000 depending on length, depth and whether we can bore instead of trench. The written estimate comes after the leak is located and before any repair begins. Permit fees are listed separately.

    Permitting and Code Compliance in Aurora, CO

    Most jurisdictions want a permit for new gas pipe or a moved line, though a straight connector swap often does not need one. We tell you up front which way your job falls. The fee goes on the estimate. The work follows Colorado adopted fuel gas code and any local amendments. The signed inspection card goes to you, and a future buyer's inspector will likely ask to see it.

    Appliance Connector Replacement in Aurora

    Old uncoated brass connectors, the kind made before the mid-1970s, are the subject of consumer safety warnings because the flare nut can separate from the tube. Any of those still in service should be replaced. We see them weekly. In its place goes a coated stainless connector and a new shutoff valve, plus a drip leg if the code calls for it. Bubble testing follows before the pilot is lit.
