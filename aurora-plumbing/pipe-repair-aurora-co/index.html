    Frequently Asked Questions About Pipe Repair in Aurora

    Minor leak repairs usually run $150 to $500. Bigger jobs cost more. Burst pipes inside finished walls or slab leaks can reach $1,000 to $4,000, depending on the pipe material, where the damage is and how much access the plumber needs to reach it. We price it after we find it.

    Most pipe repairs take one to three hours. Walls slow it down. Repairs requiring wall or ceiling access may run longer, with drywall patching handled after the pipe is tested. You hear the schedule up front.

    A simple accessible leak can be fixed in an hour or two. Hidden bursts run longer. Replacing a section of a main line or fixing a burst pipe hidden in a wall may run most of a day once access is factored in. You get a timeline first.

    Unheated areas need pipe insulation. Keep pressure in range. Hard water treatment, a working pressure regulator and good drainage around the foundation in Colorado reduce the stress that leads to leaks. Periodic inspections help.

    Stains on walls or ceilings are the most obvious sign. So does a damp odor. A drop in pressure, the sound of running water when nothing is on, a bill that jumps for no reason, or damp spots in the yard can all point to a leaking pipe. Brown water means corrosion.

    Policies often cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe. Worn pipe is usually excluded. The cost of fixing old or corroded pipe is usually yours, but the resulting damage to the house may be claimable. Check with your agent. We provide documentation.

    Clamp-on patches exist for emergencies. Think of them as a bandage. On pressurized water lines they rarely hold for long, and a DIY repair that does not meet code can fail inside a wall and cause far more damage than the original leak. Have a plumber replace it soon after.
